## Ranking Competing Explanations

The price pattern any cause must explain is sourced, not assumed. SOX fell 4.98% on 18 August and another 2.12% on 19 August (−6.99% from the 17 August close). Equal-weight S&P (RSP) was about −0.30% from 13–19 August and *rose* on Wednesday. Energy gained about 1.8% on Tuesday. Gold miners fell with yields on Tuesday and ripped after the long-end buyback on Wednesday. Mag7 was mixed, not uniformly down. [Reuters via The Business Times](https://www.businesstimes.com.sg/companies-markets/capital-markets-currencies/us-stocks-tech-selloff-weighs-down-wall-street-bond-yields-climb) (accessed 2026-08-20, confidence: High) **Established**; [Barchart 19 Aug](https://www.barchart.com/story/news/3937543/stocks-finish-mixed-as-bond-yields-fall-and-chipmakers-slide) (accessed 2026-08-20, confidence: High) **Established**.

**1. Duration / long-end yields (initiating cause).** This is the explanation established desks put first most often, and it is the only mechanism that, in one step, maps a long-end bond shock onto growth names while equal-weight barely moved. Reuters opened the 18 August close with semiconductors leading “as Middle East uncertainty pushed bond yields to multiyear peaks,” then stated the valuation channel: rising borrowing costs “lowered how much investors were willing to pay for potential growth in technology profits.” Burns McKinney of NFJ said “every time bond yields rise, that tends to disproportionately hit the technology names”; Tony Welch of SignatureFD said “there’s nothing that can crack a momentum rally quite like interest rates moving higher.” [Reuters via The Business Times](https://www.businesstimes.com.sg/companies-markets/capital-markets-currencies/us-stocks-tech-selloff-weighs-down-wall-street-bond-yields-climb) (accessed 2026-08-20, confidence: High) **Established**. CNN recorded the 30-year at 5.34%, its highest since 2007, and said a yield surge “can also put pressure on the stock market” by altering “analysts’ calculations for the value of stocks.” [CNN Business](https://www.cnn.com/2026/08/18/investing/global-bond-market) (accessed 2026-08-20, confidence: High) **Established**. AP recorded the 10-year edging *down* on Tuesday to 4.70% from 4.72% even as the 30-year stayed near its 2007 high, which means the equity hit coincided with the long end, not with a parallel backup in the policy-sensitive 10-year. [AP 18 Aug](https://apnews.com/article/stock-markets-oil-war-iran-trump-a805333e767251279e68e0686b28a8c9) (accessed 2026-08-20, confidence: High) **Established**. Official CMT closes peaked on 17 August (30-year 5.31%, 10-year 4.72%), not on 18 August (5.28% and 4.71%); the ~5.34% print is an on-the-run/intraday quote. [U.S. Treasury daily par yield curve, August 2026](https://home.treasury.gov/resource-center/data-chart-center/interest-rates/TextView?field_tdr_date_value_month=202608&type=daily_treasury_yield_curve) (accessed 2026-08-20, confidence: High) **Primary**. AP’s Tuesday lede puts AI valuation/ROI ahead of yields; Reuters, CNBC, and Barchart put yields (sparked by oil/Iran) first. Those are not the same ranking. This report scores by explanatory power for the SOX/RSP/energy pattern, not by lede frequency alone.

## Strategic Recommendations

1. **Treat 18–19 August as a semiconductor-and-memory event nested in a duration shock, not as “tech broke.”** Rationale: Composite −1.3% versus SOX ~−7% peak-to-trough, RSP ~flat, Apple/Microsoft up Tuesday, MAGS up Wednesday. Decisions that lump QQQ, Mag7, and SOXX as one factor will mis-read the tape. Evidence: Reuters 18 Aug wrap; Yahoo SOX/RSP/MAGS bars; Barchart 19 Aug.

2. **Watch the 30-year and real yields, not the Fed-funds path, as the near-term multiple for this cohort.** Rationale: Tuesday’s hit coincided with a 19-year 30-year high while the 10-year eased into the close; Wednesday’s index bounce tracked the long-end reversal; minutes did not lift hike odds. If the 30-year holds near 5.3% and chips recover, that would weaken the duration channel; if chips keep falling while the long end eases, crowded positioning is doing more of the work. Evidence: Treasury CMT; AP 18 Aug; Barchart 19 Aug; Fed minutes.

3. **Do not use $1.5 trillion or $3 trillion as 2026 bond issuance when discussing crowding.** Rationale: those figures are lease-commitment and off-balance-sheet obligation stocks. The sourced 2026 issuance cluster is $120 billion net USD IG (PIMCO) to ~$194–250 billion gross hyperscaler bonds (LSEG/Goldman). Overstating supply produces the wrong policy implication (that buybacks must offset trillions). Evidence: CNBC 14 Aug; Reuters/LSEG 29 Jul; PIMCO 10 Aug; Breakingviews ~5 bp.

4. **Separate three clocks: late-July cycle low, 13 August rebound peak, 18–19 August acute tape.** Rationale: several Mag7 fundamental disappointments (Meta, Tesla, Apple guide, Alphabet FCF) printed weeks earlier and were not Tuesday’s lede. OpenAI’s Q2 numbers are an 18 August *report* and a 19 August *residual*, not the Tuesday SOX crash. Nvidia (26 Aug) and Broadcom (2 Sep) are still ahead. Evidence: company IR/CNBC earnings wraps; CNBC 19 Aug OpenAI/Marvell coverage.

5. **Treat Thursday 20 August as a mixed macro session (oil, Iran “economic warfare,” Walmart, fading Bessent-put), not automatically as day three of a chip liquidation — then re-check the cash close.** Rationale: midday 20 August last prints had the Composite weaker and SOX roughly mixed-to-green versus Wednesday in the earlier snapshot; wraps led with yields retracing and Walmart, not Sandisk. Confidence on 20 August is Medium until the cash close exists. Evidence: CNBC 20 Aug live blog; Sharecast; Invezz.
