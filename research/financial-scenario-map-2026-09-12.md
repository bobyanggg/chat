## Scenario Map

Horizon: 2026–2030. The object is not “will US stocks fall” but “would a BRICS/SCO de-dollarization *cause* a US equity crash,” where crash means a ≥20 percent peak-to-trough decline on a major US index (bear) or a ≥30 percent decline or 1987/2008-style liquidity freeze (crash). Rankings below are labeled **synthesis**. No official house, IMF, Fed, or BIS note assigns a numeric probability that BRICS or the SCO produce either threshold by 2030. Findings are tagged **Signal** (observed), **Forecast** (named house or official projection with stated assumptions), or **Speculative** (plausible stack not anchored as anyone’s expected path).

### Bear Case (no BRICS-caused crash)

The bear case for the crash claim is that de-dollarization continues as a slow corridor project and that any US equity decline that does occur is produced by something else. That reading is the one that matches the official and academic stock-take as of 12 September 2026. **Signal.** Governor Waller opened his February 2024 dollar speech by quoting a 2011 headline, “Why the Dollar’s Reign Is Near an End,” and then said he does not expect the dollar to lose reserve status “anytime soon, nor even see a significant decline in its primacy in trade and finance.” [Federal Reserve, Waller](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/speech/waller20240215a.htm) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary**. In a May 2024 follow-up he added that a dramatic pullback by central banks and sovereign wealth funds from Treasuries is “not likely, although the growth in their demand may slow.” [Federal Reserve, Waller on r*](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/speech/waller20240524a.htm) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary**. The IMF’s July 2025 External Sector Report describes dollar dominance as “broadly stable in the 2000s, although with a moderate decrease since 2015,” and treats that decline as a reserve-share drift, not a payments or invoicing collapse. [IMF ESR 2025, ch. 2](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/esr/2025/english/ch2.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary**. IMF COFER put the dollar at 57.13 percent of allocated FX reserves in 2026Q1, the euro at 20.03 percent, and the renminbi at 1.99 percent. [IMF Data Brief, July 2026](https://data.imf.org/en/news/imf%20data%20brief%20july%201) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary**.

The same window that produced the slow reserve-share fade also produced large US equity *gains*, which is the out-of-sample test the crash claim has already failed. The dollar’s reserve share fell from about 71 percent around 1999–2001 to the high-50s by 2024–26 while the S&P compounded through two decades of bull markets interrupted by crashes that sources attribute to leverage, oil, inflation, or policy tightening, not to a BRICS reserve revolt. [IMF WP/22/58](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/wp/2022/english/wpiea2022058-print-pdf.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary**. [Damodaran historical returns](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/datafile/histretSP.html) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Established**. Named 2010–2015 de-dollarization crash calls — Rickards’ “imminent” dollar collapse and SDR transition, the 2015 SDR-basket “D-Day” newsletters — missed on timing while US equities rose. Those sources are **Low** as forecasts; they are useful only as a record of a repeatedly failed category. [ABC News / Rickards, 2011](https://abcnews.com/Business/james-rickards-sees-currency-wars-destroying-dollar/story?id=15236193) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: Medium) **Low**. [Stock Gumshoe on 2015 SDR D-Day](https://www.stockgumshoe.com/reviews/strategic-intelligence/is-september-30-really-d-day-for-the-u-s-dollar-as-jim-rickards-is-warning/) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: Medium) **Low**. Waller himself used the 2011 “reign is near an end” headline as evidence that the genre does not age well. **Signal.**

Flow prints through mid-2026 point the same way. Prasad, Liao, and Zhang told the Kansas City Fed Jackson Hole symposium that “diversification away from the dollar and from U.S. financial assets (which are not necessarily the same thing) is not borne out in the data,” and that since 2023 “net inflows in all categories have been strongly [positive]” so that “the world is increasing its allocation of external wealth to them.” [Prasad, Liao and Zhang, KC Fed, August 2026](https://www.kansascityfed.org/documents/18555/prasad.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary**. The BIS September 2025 Quarterly Review found that April 2025 selling of US assets “reversed in May and June” and that “any significant portfolio shift away from US assets is likely to be gradual” because holdings are large and strategic mandates move slowly. [BIS QR, September 2025](https://www.bis.org/publ/qtrpdf/r_qt2509.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary**. [BIS box](https://www.bis.org/publications/safe-haven-properties-us-treasuries-and-portfolio-flows-global-investors) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary**. Eichengreen, Arslanalp, and Simpson-Bell conclude that BRICS expansion, a BRICS currency, and mBridge-style platforms are “not likely to have a first-order short-run impact” on the reserve system and “not likely to have an immediate impact on the evolution of the international monetary and reserve system in the immediate future.” [NBER WP 34478](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w34478/w34478.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Established**.

This suggests the bear case is not “stocks cannot fall.” It is that the *cause* in the title does not produce the crash. CAPE near 41, a Fitch AI-bust stress test, a garden-variety recession, or a completed Fed-independence break can all generate ≥20 percent without a BRICS reserve switch. Those paths are scored below as mis-attribution risk, not as evidence for the crash claim. **Synthesis.**

### Base Case

The consensus trajectory through 2030 is a still-first dollar, a slow reserve-share drift, and a US equity market that can cheapen or rotate without a BRICS-caused crash. Goldman’s Trivedi–Jenkins note is the cleanest house statement of that split: “The Dollar will not be replaced anytime soon, but it should still depreciate.” Their mechanism is “a cheapening of US local assets or a weakening currency, most likely both” as an equilibrating response to less-exceptional US growth and a large current-account deficit — a valuation reset, not a crash, and “not contingent on an erosion in Dollar dominance.” They write that evidence of true de-dollarization is “still limited” and that they “do not expect this to contribute to the Dollar’s performance on a day-to-day, or even year-to-year, basis.” [Goldman Sachs via CFS / Trivedi–Jenkins](https://www.cfsrating.com/media/ujigt4nb/global-markets-analyst_-dollar-dominance-and-dollar-depreciation-moving-on-different-tracks-trivedi_jenkins.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary**. [Goldman, Different Dollar Downside, January 2026](https://www.gspublishing.com/content/research/en/reports/2026/01/10/3d5c48b3-164b-4ba7-a9f1-c2c2adf17f64.html) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary**. **Forecast.**

J.P. Morgan Long-Term Strategy says *if* structural de-dollarization occurred, “outright and relative returns [on US equities] would be negatively impacted by divestment or reallocation away from U.S. markets and a severe loss in confidence,” with “upward pressure on real yields.” The same house states that evidence for a structural shift “remains limited,” that “suggestions of the dollar’s demise are premature,” and that each 1-percentage-point decline in foreign Treasury holdings relative to GDP (about $300 billion) would lift yields by more than 33 basis points — “this is not our base case.” [J.P. Morgan](https://www.jpmorgan.com/insights/global-research/currencies/de-dollarization) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary**. J.P. Morgan Asset Management’s March 2026 *Eye on the Market* likewise treats April 2025 “Sell America” as a failed self-feeding trade: after the episode, the trade-weighted dollar was little changed, US long yields rose less than other developed-market yields, and US equities outperformed. [J.P. Morgan AM](https://am.jpmorgan.com/content/dam/jpm-am-aem/global/en/insights/eye-on-the-market/semiquincententacles-amv.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary**. **Forecast.**

BlackRock Investment Institute, 13 October 2025, wrote that the 2025 dollar drop “sparked questions about its haven status and a pullback from U.S. assets. We haven’t seen evidence of that so far.” They attributed about half of the slide to expected Fed cuts, the rest to a return of term premium from global debt concerns, and concluded: “We stay overweight U.S. stocks.” [BlackRock](https://www.blackrock.com/us/individual/literature/market-commentary/weekly-investment-commentary-en-us-20251013-us-dollar-drop-not-that-unusual-yet.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary**. **Forecast.** Vanguard’s 2026 outlook is the valuation-drag variant of the same consensus: near-term US equity returns can still be solid on earnings, but five-to-ten-year expected returns on US growth are muted and the better risk-return profiles sit in high-quality US fixed income, US value, and non-US developed equities. That is a rotation and a lower-return regime, not a BRICS crash path. [Vanguard VEMO 2026](https://corporate.vanguard.com/content/corporatesite/us/en/corp/vemo/2026-outlook-economic-upside-stock-market-downside.html) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Established**. **Forecast.**

Eichengreen’s 2023–26 line is “no dollar crisis or collapse, only a gradual erosion,” with the binding risk on the US side — soaring debts, another debt-ceiling showdown, and “getting its economic and political act together” — rather than on BRICS cohesion. [Eichengreen, Project Syndicate, 11 September 2023](https://www.project-syndicate.org/commentary/will-us-dollar-global-hegemony-last-by-barry-eichengreen-2023-09) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Established**. [Eichengreen, NY Fed, May 2023](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/conference/2023/international-roles-usdollar/day2/eichengreen-bsr-frbny-may2023.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Established**. Prasad’s 2024 *Foreign Affairs* piece is the same architecture: the dollar is “hostage to politics,” a tipping point *could* come if markets decide the US debt path is unsustainable or if Fed independence and the rule of law break, but “until that disparity [in alternatives] changes… don’t expect the dollar to decline.” [Prasad, *Foreign Affairs*](https://www.foreignaffairs.com/united-states/top-dollar-currency-prasad) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Established**. Krishnamurthy’s complete-disappearance counterfactual is the academic upper bound that still is not a crash forecast: an 8.8 percent real dollar depreciation and about 90 basis points on US real rates, with the explicit caveat that “Losing reserve currency status is not like a bank run.” [Jiang, Krishnamurthy, Lustig and Richmond, NBER w35328](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w35328/w35328.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Established**. [Stanford GSB](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/insights/dollars-dominance-eroding) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Established**. The paper does not publish an S&P percent. **Forecast** (model counterfactual), not a 2030 path.

Hartnett’s “Anything But Dollar” is a rotation and dollar-debasement hedge — long gold, long international, short the dollar — driven by US fiscal and policy-rate politics, not a numbered BRICS-to-S&P crash. [CNBC / Hartnett, April 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/17/bank-of-americas-hartnett-backs-two-trades-to-play-lower-rates-ai-war.html) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: Medium) **Established**. **Forecast.**

This suggests the base case for 2026–2030 is: dollar first on every major metric; reserve share drifting toward the mid-50s if OMFIF’s reserve-manager survey is the right compass; US assets possibly cheaper in currency or multiple terms; no official or bulge-bracket S&P drawdown tied to a Treasury dump or a BRICS reserve revolt. [OMFIF, 22 October 2024](https://www.omfif.org/2024/10/how-gradual-decline-in-dollar-dominance-could-quicken/) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Established**. **Synthesis** on the “no crash from this cause” clause; **Forecast** on the house dollar and allocation views.

### Bull Case (crash from this cause)

A crash *from this cause* is a stacked-shock path, not the continuation of observed de-dollarization. No Fed, BIS, IMF, Goldman, J.P. Morgan, BlackRock, Eichengreen, Prasad, or Krishnamurthy note treats that stack as the expected 2026–2030 trajectory. What follows is therefore **Speculative** on occurrence and **synthesis** on the arithmetic that would be required for ≥20 percent or ≥30 percent. The five conditions named in the literature as accelerants are US fiscal credibility, a Fed-independence break, a Gulf savings dump, an Indian strategic flip, and Chinese capital-account convertibility. Each is documented as a *watch item*. None is documented as having fired in a way that produced a US equity crash.

**What the published elasticities actually buy.** Gormsen and Lazarus estimate US equity cash-flow duration of about 20 years and warn that arbitrary rate changes do not move equities one-for-one with duration; only a “pure discounting” shock does. That duration arithmetic implies that a 100 basis-point *pure* discount-rate shock, cash-flow growth and the equity risk premium held fixed, is on the order of a 20 percent index move, and 150 basis points on the order of 30 percent. [Gormsen and Lazarus, NBER w34814](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w34814/w34814.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Established**. Krishnamurthy et al.’s complete loss of reserve demand is +90 basis points on real rates and −8.8 percent on the real dollar — close to the 20 percent duration threshold *if* that 90 basis points is a pure discounting shock and cash flows are unchanged, and not close to 30 percent on rates alone. The paper’s own wealth-loss language is “roughly one year of U.S. GDP.” Secondary write-ups of the same paper disagree on the dollar figure: CEPR/VoxEU cite about $29 trillion; the NBER Digest cites more than $18 trillion. [NBER w35328](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w35328/w35328.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Established**. [CEPR / VoxEU](https://cepr.org/voxeu/columns/dollar-erosion-macroeconomic-consequences-losing-reserve-currency-status) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: Medium) **Established**. [NBER Digest, September 2026](https://www.nber.org/digest/202609/valuing-dollars-reserve-currency-status) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Established**. J.P. Morgan’s “not our base case” elasticity — more than 33 basis points per $300 billion, or 1 percent of GDP, of foreign Treasury holdings — would need on the order of $900 billion of net foreign Treasury sales to reach 100 basis points, and about $1.4 trillion to reach 150 basis points, *if* that coefficient is applied linearly, which the note does not authorize. [J.P. Morgan](https://www.jpmorgan.com/insights/global-research/currencies/de-dollarization) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary**. Neely’s St. Louis Fed scenario for sale of China’s entire then-$3 trillion reserve portfolio is only 19–32 basis points along the curve, an order of magnitude smaller. [Neely, St. Louis Fed WP 2017-001](https://files.stlouisfed.org/files/htdocs/wp/2017/2017-001.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary**. Officials held $2.23 trillion of US equities on 30 June 2025; applying Gabaix–Koijen’s domestic multiplier of 5 to a complete official equity liquidation is a roughly 21 percent price move only if that multiplier travels one-for-one to foreign official sales, which is not documented. [U.S. Treasury SB0482](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0482) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary**. [Gabaix and Koijen, NBER w28967](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w28967/w28967.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Established**. **Synthesis:** a ≥20 percent index move *from this cause* requires either a 100-plus basis-point pure discounting shock that published long-run official-sale elasticities do not deliver from any historically observed dump, or a multi-trillion-dollar net foreign *equity* sale that is not in the 2000–2026 TIC record. A ≥30 percent move needs a further increment — 150 basis points of pure discounting, or that rate shock plus a cash-flow recession, or on the order of $3 trillion of net foreign equity selling under the inelastic-markets multiplier.

**The five stacked conditions, as documented.**

(1) *US fiscal.* CBO’s February 2026 baseline already has the deficit at 5.8 percent of GDP in 2026 and debt held by the public rising from 101 percent of GDP in 2026 to 108 percent in 2030 and 120 percent in 2036. In that baseline the 10-year yield only drifts from about 4.1 percent in 2026 to 4.4 percent by 2031. [CBO, Budget and Economic Outlook 2026–2036](https://www.cbo.gov/publication/62105) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary**. **Forecast.** That path is a term-premium grind, not a crash. The fiscal *shock* that Eichengreen and Prasad name is not the CBO baseline; it is a failed auction, a debt-ceiling interruption of interest payments, or a market decision that the debt path will be inflated away. Eichengreen told a 2023 New York Fed conference that “an extended government shutdown and even a temporary interruption of interest payments on Treasuries would not exactly enhance the allure of the dollar,” citing outside estimates of a 2–3 percent probability of that event — a number this report repeats only as his citation, not as an independent probability. [Eichengreen, NY Fed, May 2023](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/conference/2023/international-roles-usdollar/day2/eichengreen-bsr-frbny-may2023.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: Medium) **Established**. Prasad writes that “there might be a tipping point at which markets decide that the level and trajectory of U.S. public debt are unsustainable” and “dump U.S. Treasury securities.” [Prasad, *Foreign Affairs*](https://www.foreignaffairs.com/united-states/top-dollar-currency-prasad) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Established**. **Speculative** as a 2026–2030 realized event; **Signal** as a named watch item.

(2) *Fed-independence break.* Prasad’s 2024 article already treated Fed independence and the rule of law as “bulwarks of foreign investors’ belief in the stable long-term value of the dollar.” The 2025–26 Cook removal fight and the Warsh transition are the live test. The Supreme Court’s June 2026 5–4 ruling blocked the first attempt to oust Governor Cook for lack of due process; Fortune and S&P both framed that ruling as protecting the Treasury market from a credibility shock. [Fortune, 29 June 2026](https://fortune.com/2026/06/29/supreme-court-fed-independence-lisa-cook-us-debt-treasury-bonds/) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Established**. A completed break — politicized easing that unanchors inflation expectations — is the channel that could turn Krishnamurthy’s 90 basis points from a multi-decade erosion into a discrete term-premium gap. Observed reactions to the 2025–26 attempts have been curve-steepening and modest dollar softness, not a 20 percent equity event. **Signal** on the fight; **Speculative** on a completed break producing a crash.

(3) *Gulf dump.* Identifiable Saudi plus UAE Treasury holdings were $142.5 billion and $114.8 billion at end-June 2026, about $257 billion combined — well under one percent of marketable Treasuries and under J.P. Morgan’s $300 billion / 33 basis-point unit. [Treasury TIC Table 5](https://ticdata.treasury.gov/resource-center/data-chart-center/tic/Documents/slt_table5.txt) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary**. March 2026 cuts of about $17 billion year-on-year still left both books above March 2025 levels. [Arab News](https://www.arabnews.com/finance/saudi-arabia-uae-cut-us-treasury-holdings-in-march-2644129) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Established**. ING’s 2026 petrodollar note is that any Gulf de-dollarization “would more likely show up through slower new inflows rather than reallocation of the existing stock,” and that GCC external finance remains dollar-heavy (69 percent of BIS-tracked international assets versus 46 percent globally). Gulf SWFs are on the order of $6 trillion; that is the object that would have to rotate to matter for a 20 percent equity hole. [ING](https://think.ing.com/articles/still-king-why-the-petrodollar-isnt-going-anywhere-soon/) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Established**. A few monthly Treasury trims are **Signal**. A coordinated SWF exit from US equities and Treasuries is **Speculative**.

(4) *India flip.* India is the consensus veto. Jaishankar said on 7 December 2024 that “India has never been for de-dollarisation,” “there is no proposal to have a BRICS currency,” and “We have no interest in weakening the dollar at all.” [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/india/brics-have-no-interest-weakening-usd-indian-foreign-minister-says-2024-12-07/) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Established**. Piyush Goyal repeated the opposition to a BRICS currency on 7 August 2026. [Economic Times](https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/news/india/india-not-in-favour-of-separate-brics-currency-piyush-goyal/articleshow/133035236.cms) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Established**. India held $186.4 billion of Treasuries at end-June 2026. [Treasury TIC Table 5](https://ticdata.treasury.gov/resource-center/data-chart-center/tic/Documents/slt_table5.txt) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary**. A flip that matters for the crash claim is not a rupee invoicing experiment; it is New Delhi joining a binding anti-dollar payments and reserve programme and selling the US book. That is off the 2026 official path. **Signal** on the veto; **Speculative** on the flip.

(5) *China convertibility.* SAFE/PBOC in June 2026 still described capital-account convertibility as a “systemic undertaking” requiring “phased implementation” and “safeguarding bottom-line security.” [SAFE](http://www.safe.gov.cn/en/2026/0617/2439.html) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary**. The IMF’s 2025 Article IV annex states that “exchange controls continue to apply to most capital transactions.” [IMF Country Report 2026/044](https://www.elibrary.imf.org/view/journals/002/2026/044/article-A002-en.xml) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary**. Eichengreen’s 2026 view is that China is building rails that do not require the dollar, but “it will take a decade and more” before the RMB can rival it. [Eichengreen, UC Berkeley](https://live-ucb-econ-drupal.pantheon.berkeley.edu/news/will-dollar-remain-worlds-dominant-currency) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Established**. Without convertibility there is no liquid challenger reserve asset for a genuine substitution crash. With a surprise opening plus a Gulf and Indian reallocation, the Krishnamurthy complete-loss counterfactual becomes a less empty thought experiment. **Signal** on controls remaining; **Speculative** on a 2026–2030 opening.

**How the stack would have to work to count as this cause.** A crash from *this cause* needs the foreign official and BRICS/SCO pieces — Gulf savings, an Indian consensus flip, and a usable RMB — to be material contributors to a 100–150 basis-point pure discounting shock or a multi-trillion equity outflow, not merely a chorus after a US-made event. US fiscal stress plus a Fed-independence break, by themselves, are US institutional shocks. Eichengreen’s 2023 line is explicit that whether the dollar keeps its role “will depend not simply on the United States’ relations with Russia, China, or the BRICS countries” but on US debts and politics. [Eichengreen, Project Syndicate](https://www.project-syndicate.org/commentary/will-us-dollar-global-hegemony-last-by-barry-eichengreen-2023-09) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Established**. If those US shocks produce a ≥20 percent equity decline, BRICS summits will claim credit. The causal label would still be US self-harm. **Synthesis.**

**Mis-attribution: AI-bust and recession crashes that will be blamed on BRICS.** Fitch Ratings on 9 September 2026 published a *severe equity-price shock* stress test, not a reserve-status forecast. Business Insider’s recap describes a 35 percent drop in *AI-sector* prices over six months, a 15 percent drop in China/Japan/UK equities, a 2027 US recession, and an explicit note that this “was not their base case for the AI trade.” Secondary recaps collapse that into a 35 percent *US stock* crash. Sources disagree on the perimeter of the 35 percent; they agree it is an AI-capex re-evaluation, not a BRICS dump. [Business Insider / Fitch](https://www.businessinsider.com/ai-stock-crash-us-recession-global-stagnation-fitch-ratings-2026-9) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Established**. [Fitch Ratings, 9 September 2026](https://www.fitchratings.com/research/sovereigns/severe-equity-price-shock-scenario-would-entail-us-recession-09-09-2026) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: Medium) **Primary** (title and date confirmed; full note is paywalled). **Forecast** (stress test). The two worst recent US bears, 2008 and 2022, came with a *stronger* dollar and are attributed to a financial-system crash and a domestic inflation/tightening cycle. [McCauley and McGuire, BIS](https://www.bis.org/publications/dollar-appreciation-2008-safe-haven-carry-trades-dollar-shortage-and-overhedging_1.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary**. [Reuters, 2022](https://www.reuters.com/markets/us/futures-slip-last-trading-day-torrid-year-2022-12-30/) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Established**. April 2025’s dollar-down-plus-stocks-down episode was a US tariff shock, recovered in weeks, and is the BIS’s exhibit that a “Sell America” flow reversed. [San Francisco Fed](https://www.frbsf.org/research-and-insights/publications/economic-letter/2025/10/market-reactions-to-tariff-announcements/) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary**. [BIS QR, September 2025](https://www.bis.org/publ/qtrpdf/r_qt2509.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary**. A 2027–2030 recession or AI-earnings bust that coincides with a Kazan- or New Delhi-style communiqué will be narrated as de-dollarization. The historical sign of the dollar in true risk-off crashes, and Fitch’s own stated driver, say otherwise.

**Falsifiable markers that would move this axis off the base case.** A ≥20 percent claim from this cause would need, in the same window: a measured 100-plus basis-point *pure* discounting shock that Fed/BIS/IMF event studies attribute to official or BRICS selling rather than to Fed policy or a growth scare; and/or TIC-documented net foreign equity sales on the order of $2 trillion that are not a valuation residual. A ≥30 percent claim from this cause would need the 150 basis-point version of that rate shock, or that shock plus a cash-flow recession whose first cause is the same reserve revolt, or on the order of $3 trillion of identified net foreign equity selling. Those markers are not in the 2000–2026 official record. Until they appear, the bull case remains a stacked-shock thought experiment that the named literature refuses to treat as a forecast.

Morgan >33 bp per $300 billion versus Beltran short-run 40–60 bp per $100 billion monthly flow). Krishnamurthy wealth-loss recaps of the same paper disagree ($18 trillion vs $29 trillion vs “one year of GDP”).
