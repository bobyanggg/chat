## Momentum and Adjacent Forces

### USD–Equity Correlation and Offsets

This axis documents the *sign* of the U.S. dollar versus the S&P 500 from the end of Bretton Woods through September 2026, and the measured offsets that keep a weaker dollar from mapping one-for-one into an equity crash. Date of research: 2026-09-12. Source types are tagged **Primary** (Fed, Treasury, BIS, S&P DJI, FOMC staff), **Established** (NBER or widely cited academic papers; Damodaran’s compiled total-return series), or **Low** (vendor blogs — not used for elasticities). Episode signs and published coefficients are labeled **fact**. Sentences that apply those facts to a hypothetical BRICS/SCO official sale are labeled **synthesis**.

The long-run USD–S&P relationship is not a stable negative number that would make “dollar down ⇒ stocks crash” an empirical regularity. The sign flips with the *shock*. Global risk-off and Fed tightening typically send the dollar up and equities down. Orderly dollar depreciation under easy or stable U.S. financial conditions typically accompanies rising S&P earnings and prices. The two fall together when the shock is a U.S. policy or U.S.-risk event that hits both the currency’s convenience yield and the equity cash-flow path — April 2025 is the documented modern case. Vendor papers that report a single sample correlation (for example −0.51 on 2016–2023 daily closes, or −0.098 on 2013–2023 returns) are **Low** relative to the user’s source bar and are not used as the axis estimate. [ResearchGate / *Advances in Economics, Management and Political Sciences*](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/385435569_The_Research_of_the_Correlation_Between_USD_Index_and_SP500) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: Low) **Low.** [SJEMR](http://www.sjemr.org/download/SJEMR-6-2-1-7.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: Low) **Low.**

**1971–1984.** After the 15 August 1971 Nixon shock the next session was a rally, not a crash; calendar-1971 S&P total return was **+14.22 percent**. **Fact.** [New York Fed *Monthly Review*](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/monthly_review/1971_pdf/09_2_71.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary.** [Damodaran](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/datafile/histretSP.html) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Established.** The 1973–74 bear (S&P total returns **−14.31** and **−25.90** percent) did overlap a weak post-Bretton Woods dollar, so this is one window in which the two *can* fall together. Official Fed History attributes that bear to oil, Vietnam financing, food prices, and the preceding boom — not to a foreign official dump of dollar assets. **Fact (returns and official attribution).** [Damodaran](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/datafile/histretSP.html) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Established.** [Federal Reserve History](https://www.federalreservehistory.org/essays/oil-shock-of-1973-74) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary.**

**1985–87 (Plaza).** Frankel’s reconstruction of the Plaza Accord records that the Fed’s major-currency dollar index rose from 93 in September 1980 to 144 at the February 1985 peak (1973=100), then “came back down **40 percent**” in 1985–87. **Fact.** [Frankel, Baker Institute / NBER line](https://www.bakerinstitute.org/sites/default/files/2015-09/import/WorkingPaper-Plaza-Frankel-092815.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Established.** S&P total returns in those calendar years were **+31.24**, **+18.49**, and **+5.81** percent — a three-year compound of about **+65 percent** while the dollar was being talked down. **Fact.** [Damodaran](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/datafile/histretSP.html) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Established.** The October 19, 1987 crash is the one-day exception inside that window: the S&P 500 fell about **20 percent** that session. Carlson’s Fed staff history lists rising global rates, an above-consensus August trade-deficit print, a declining dollar, and the inference that the Fed might tighten — plus market-structure failures — as the proximate mix. The Federal Reserve then issued a public liquidity statement, pushed the funds rate down from over **7.5 percent** to around **7 percent**, and injected reserves for weeks. Calendar 1987 still closed positive. **Fact.** [Carlson, Fed FEDS 2007-13](https://www.federalreserve.gov/pubs/feds/2007/200713/200713pap.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary.** **Synthesis:** Plaza is the existence proof that a large, policy-sponsored dollar decline need not crash the S&P; 19 October 1987 is the existence proof that a *dollar-slide-plus-tightening-scare* can coincide with a one-day crash, and that the Fed’s liquidity backstop was used.

**2002–08.** CRS, using the Fed’s price-adjusted broad dollar index, records a **26 percent** real trade-weighted depreciation from the early-2002 peak to mid-2008, then an **11 percent** appreciation over the next nine crisis months. MAS, using the Fed’s *nominal* broad and major-currency indexes, reports **−26 percent** and **−37 percent** from February 2002 to end-March 2008. Jiang, Krishnamurthy, Lustig, and related work put the advanced-foreign-economy dollar index at **−31.7 percent** in 2002–07 and **+9.3 percent** in 2008. **Fact.** [CRS RL34582](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/20110415_RL34582_bdf52352969d87f944d4996278ff281c78ebdfd3.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary.** [MAS *Macroeconomic Review*](https://www.mas.gov.sg/-/media/mas/monetary-policy-and-economics/education-and-research/research/economics-essays/2008-apr/mrapr08_special-feature-a_usd.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Established.** [Jiang et al. circulating AFA paper](https://afajof.org/management/viewp.php?n=36008) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: Medium) **Established.** S&P total returns were **−21.97** percent in 2002 (the last year of the tech bear, overlapping the *start* of the dollar’s descent), then **+28.36, +10.74, +4.83, +15.61, +5.48** percent in 2003–07 (about **+82 percent** compounded) and **−36.55** percent in 2008. **Fact.** [Damodaran](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/datafile/histretSP.html) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Established.** McCauley and McGuire document that the 2008 dollar *rise* was a flight into Treasury bills, carry-trade reversal, and a dollar-funding shortage — the opposite of a reserve dump. **Fact.** [McCauley and McGuire, BIS *Quarterly Review*, Dec 2009](https://www.bis.org/publications/dollar-appreciation-2008-safe-haven-carry-trades-dollar-shortage-and-overhedging_1.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary.**

**2011.** The second-half European sovereign and U.S. downgrade window is a risk-off, *stronger*-dollar episode. The New York Fed’s Q3 2011 FX operations report records a **5.3 percent** appreciation of the Fed major-currencies index, an **8.3 percent** rise against the euro, and a **6.7 percent** S&P drop on 8 August, the first session after the S&P sovereign downgrade. Treasury’s year-end FX report puts the dollar **+6.8 percent** against other major currencies from end-June to mid-December. Calendar-2011 S&P total return was still **+2.10 percent**. **Fact.** [NY Fed FX operations, Q3 2011](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/newsevents/news/markets/2011/fxq311.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary.** [U.S. Treasury FX Report 2011](https://home.treasury.gov/system/files/206/FXReport2011.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary.** [Damodaran](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/datafile/histretSP.html) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Established.** A CME note that the S&P shed **19.4 percent** from end-April to early October is a vendor peak-to-trough print, not a Fed or S&P DJI series; it is consistent in *sign* with the official Q3 account. [CME Group](https://www.cmegroup.com/insights/economic-research/2023/debt-ceiling-lessons-from-the-2011-budget-debate.html) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: Medium) **Established (vendor).**

**2022.** This is the clean modern case of a *stronger* dollar and a ≥20 percent equity drawdown arriving together. BIS Bulletin 62 records that the broad dollar had appreciated about **10 percent** from the start of 2022 to the bulletin’s date and was at a four-decade high. The New York Fed’s Q4 2022 FX operations report states that the broad trade-weighted dollar appreciated **10.5 percent** in the first three quarters and then depreciated **4.7 percent** in Q4 as Fed-tightening expectations eased. **Fact.** [BIS Bulletin 62](https://www.bis.org/publ/bisbull62.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary.** [NY Fed FX operations, Q4 2022](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/newsevents/news/markets/2022/fxq422.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary.** S&P DJI’s year-end 2025 attributes table records a **19.44 percent** 2022 *price* decline (**−18.11 percent** with dividends). Silverblatt’s June 13, 2022 bear-market call used S&P DJI’s **20 percent** closing-peak rule and a then-print of **−21.8 percent** from the January 3 close of 4,796.56. Damodaran’s 2022 total return is **−18.04 percent**. **Fact.** [S&P DJI, U.S. Equities Market Attributes, Dec 2025](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/commentary/article/us-equities-market-attributes/) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary.** [CNBC citing Silverblatt](https://www.cnbc.com/2022/06/13/sp-500-is-in-official-bear-market-according-to-sp-dow-jones-indices.html) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Established.** [Damodaran](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/datafile/histretSP.html) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Established.** FactSet, using a **40 percent** international-revenue working number, recorded that **50 percent** of early-reporting S&P 500 Q3 2022 calls cited a negative FX impact — the opposite of a weak-dollar crash channel. **Fact.** [FactSet](https://insight.factset.com/50-of-sp-500-companies-citing-a-negative-impact-from-fx-on-q3-earnings-calls-to-date) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Established.**

**2025.** April 2–4 is the documented modern case of the dollar and the S&P *falling together*. The San Francisco Fed’s event study records an **11 percent** two-day S&P drop, a **6–8 percent** decline in S&P dividend futures out to three years, and dollar *depreciation* against the Swiss franc, yen, and euro (and *appreciation* against the Australian and New Zealand dollars). The authors interpret the pattern as a portfolio reallocation away from the United States after a U.S. tariff shock, not as a BRICS reserve switch. **Fact.** [San Francisco Fed Economic Letter 2025-23](https://www.frbsf.org/research-and-insights/publications/economic-letter/2025/10/market-reactions-to-tariff-announcements/) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary.** BIS Bulletin 105 labels the same weeks a “triple decline” in equities, bonds, and the dollar, and attributes the dollar slide to *ex-post FX hedging* of unhedged dollar assets — concentrated in Asian hours, when Treasury prices *rose*. **Fact (authors’ high-frequency interpretation).** [BIS Bulletin 105](https://www.bis.org/publ/bisbull105.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary.** The calendar year then reversed the April sign: S&P DJI records a **16.39 percent** 2025 price gain (**17.88 percent** with dividends; Damodaran **+17.78 percent**) and, in the same note, “the dollar on the low side—not unassociated” with strong domestic and international equity inflows. VIX spiked to **60.13** on April 7 and closed the year at **15.01**. Year-end S&P 500 market value was **$58.438 trillion**. **Fact.** [S&P DJI, Dec 2025](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/commentary/article/us-equities-market-attributes/) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary.** [Damodaran](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/datafile/histretSP.html) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Established.**

ECB staff (Georgiadis, Müller, and Schumann) identify a global risk shock over 1990–2019 that *appreciates* the dollar and *lowers* equity prices — the 2008/2011/2022 sign. **Fact.** [ECB WP 2628](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/pub/pdf/scpwps/ecb.wp2628~1f46b7ba26.en.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary.** That is why a weaker dollar is so often *not* the crash state: the historically crash-capable shock (global risk-off) bids the dollar.

**Offset 1 — Mag7 / AI earnings and FX translation.** S&P DJI’s own foreign-sales reports put S&P 500 foreign sales at **42.90 percent** in 2018 (**43.62** in 2017; recent high **47.82** in 2014; recent low **41.84** in 2003). Information Technology — the Mag7’s sector — was **58.19 percent** foreign in 2018. **Fact.** [S&P DJI, “S&P 500 2018: Global Sales”](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/documents/research/sp-500-global-sales-2018.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary.** Federal Reserve staff (Lansing and others, FEDS 2024-049) estimate, on 1993–2023 quarterly S&P earnings, that **1 percent** dollar *appreciation* reduces profit growth by about **¾ of a percent**, with the dollar entering through translation, export prices, and a foreign-growth signal. Macro variables including the dollar explain **25–45 percent** of the variation in S&P earnings growth. **Fact.** [Federal Reserve FEDS 2024-049](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/files/2024049pap.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary.** S&P DJI’s December 2025 attributes note states that the Magnificent 7 contributed **55 percent** of the three-year total return (an **88.11 percent** three-year gain would have been **39 percent** without them) and that 2025’s **17.9 percent** total return would have been **10.4 percent** without the group; NVIDIA and Microsoft together were **14 percent** of index market value. **Fact.** [S&P DJI, Dec 2025](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/commentary/article/us-equities-market-attributes/) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary.** LSEG I/B/E/S attributes **52 percent** of 2024 S&P net earnings growth to the Mag-7 (expected to fall to **33 percent** in 2025). FactSet’s later 2025–26 prints still have Mag-7 earnings growth in the low-20s percent against low-to-mid-teens for the other 493. **Fact.** [LSEG / Lipper](https://lipperalpha.refinitiv.com/2025/03/magnificent-7-q4-2024-earnings-review-growth-holds-but-rotation-awaits/) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: Medium) **Established.** [FactSet](https://insight.factset.com/are-magnificent-7-companies-still-top-contributors-to-earnings-growth-for-the-sp-500-for-q4) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: Medium) **Established.** Gopinath’s pass-through estimates keep the *inflation* side of a weaker dollar small: a **10 percent** dollar depreciation raises U.S. CPI about **0.4–0.7 percentage points** over two years. **Fact.** [NBER Digest / Gopinath w21646](https://www.nber.org/digest/jan16/cross-country-differences-exchange-rate-effects-inflation) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Established.** **Synthesis:** a 10 percent BRICS-associated dollar decline would, on the Fed staff coefficient, add on the order of **7–8 percent** to S&P earnings growth and would not, on Gopinath, recreate 2022-sized inflation. That is an *earnings tailwind*, concentrated in the same global-revenue names that already supplied more than half of recent index return. It is not a bid for dumped Treasuries.

**Offset 2 — domestic household bid.** Households and nonprofits held **$64.800 trillion** of corporate equities, directly and indirectly, at Q1 2026 (Q4 2025: **$66.618 trillion**). **Fact.** [FRED BOGZ1LM153064475Q](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/BOGZ1LM153064475Q) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary.** [Federal Reserve Z.1, June 11, 2026](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/z1/current/recent_developments.htm) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary.** Residual household *transactions* in corporate equities were **$215.3, $3.2, $378.6, $266.4, and $330.6 billion** in 2025:Q1–2026:Q1, or about **$864 billion** in calendar 2025 and **$1.19 trillion** over five quarters. **Fact.** [ALFRED / Z.1 FU153064105](https://alfred.stlouisfed.org/series?seid=BOGZ1FU153064105Q) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary.** The Z.1 household equity *flow* is a residual after other sectors are subtracted; most of the quarter-to-quarter change in the **$65 trillion** stock is revaluation (Q1 2026 revaluation **−$1.996 trillion** against transactions of **+$331 billion**). **Fact.** [FRED BOGZ1FR153064475Q](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/BOGZ1FR153064475Q) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary.** Official foreign equity holdings were **$2.230 trillion** on 30 June 2025 against **$19.860 trillion** of all foreign equity holdings; core-BRICS equity in the TIC country table is about **$429 billion**. Foreign official *net purchases* of U.S. equities were still **+$114.3 billion** in the twelve months through June 2026. **Fact.** [U.S. Treasury SB0482](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0482) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary.** [TIC SHL Table 1](https://ticdata.treasury.gov/Publish/shlptab1.html) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary.** [U.S. Treasury SB0606](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0606) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary.** **Synthesis:** the domestic stock is an order of magnitude larger than official foreign equity and two orders larger than core-BRICS equity. The 2025 residual *flow* is large enough, on paper, to absorb a complete core-BRICS equity book or a large slice of the official equity book — if households chose to buy the shares that officials sold, which is not identified in Z.1. It is not large enough, as a one-year flow, to absorb the **$3.500 trillion** official long-term Treasury book.

**Offset 3 — Fed put, FIMA, swap lines.** The 1987 response is the documented “Fed put” in this sample: a public liquidity statement plus open-market operations that lowered the funds rate about **50 basis points** in a day. **Fact.** [Carlson, FEDS 2007-13](https://www.federalreserve.gov/pubs/feds/2007/200713/200713pap.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary.** The standing facilities that bear on a *BRICS official sale* are not equity-buying programs. Dollar swap lines exist so foreign central banks can deliver dollars to their banks without those banks (or the official sector) selling U.S. assets into a disorderly market. NY Fed staff record peak swap-line usage of **$449 billion** in late May 2020 (GFC peak **$598 billion**); Brookings cites about **$470 billion**. **Fact; sources disagree on the 2020 peak by about $20 billion.** [Choi, Goldberg, Lerman, Ravazzolo, NY Fed *EPR* 2022](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/epr/2022/epr_2022_fima-repo_choi.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary.** [Brookings](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/what-are-federal-reserve-swap-lines/) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: Medium) **Established.** The FIMA Repo Facility, temporary from 31 March 2020 and standing from 28 July 2021, lets approved foreign official holders repo Treasuries to the Fed for overnight or seven-day dollars “other than sales of the securities in the open market.” **Fact.** [Federal Reserve, FIMA Repo Facility](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fima-repo-facility.htm) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary.** [Federal Reserve, FIMA FAQs](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fima-repo-facility-faqs.htm) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary.** Actual 2020 usage was **minimal**; a YPFS case study puts the peak at **$1.404 billion**, against official Treasury *sales* of about **$150 billion** in March 2020 and **$70 billion** in April. NY Fed staff argue the *announcement* eased precautionary selling and that official Treasury buying resumed in the second week of April. **Fact.** [Choi et al., NY Fed *EPR*](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/epr/2022/epr_2022_fima-repo_choi.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary.** [NY Fed Staff Report 983](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr983.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary.** [YPFS / SSRN case](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4509407) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: Medium) **Established.** **Synthesis:** FIMA and the swaps can absorb a *liquidity-driven* official Treasury sale by converting it into a Fed repo or a central-bank dollar loan. They do not absorb a *permanent* reserve-composition switch: an official that wants gold, renminbi, or a BRICS unit must eventually sell the Treasury, not roll a seven-day repo. The 2020 episode also shows that even with the facilities in place, officials still sold on the order of **$220 billion** of Treasuries — and U.S. equities’ crash that March was a pandemic risk-off, not a reserve revolt.

**Offset 4 — dollar stablecoins.** BIS Working Paper 1270, using daily data from January 2021 to March 2026, measures dollar-stablecoin assets under management above **$270 billion** in December 2025, of which **$153 billion** were Treasury bills — larger than some major foreign official T-bill books. Issuers bought nearly **$35 billion** of T-bills in 2025. A **$3.5 billion** (two-standard-deviation) inflow lowers the 3-month bill yield **0.71 basis points** on impact and about **4 basis points** within ten days, with **limited spillover to longer tenors**. **Fact.** [BIS WP 1270](https://www.bis.org/publications/working-paper-1270-stablecoins-and-safe-asset-prices.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary.** Brookings (Liang and Neiman) put the June 2026 stablecoin market at about **$270 billion** and note that GENIUS-eligible reserves are concentrated in short Treasuries; first-round T-bill demand scenarios to 2030 span **$400 billion to $2.3 trillion** depending on whether growth comes from abroad or from money-fund substitution. Those 2030 figures are scenarios, not 2026 stocks. **Fact (stock); scenarios labeled as such.** [Brookings](https://www.brookings.edu/articles/stablecoins-after-genius/) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Established.** **Synthesis:** stablecoins are a measured, growing bid for *dollar bills*, which reinforces the dollar’s safe-asset role at the front end and can offset a slice of official T-bill diversification. They are two orders of magnitude smaller than the **$3.5 trillion** official long-term Treasury stock, they do not bid for the S&P, and the BIS paper finds **no** identified pass-through to the 10-year yield that would protect equity duration.

**Can these offsets absorb a BRICS-sized official sale?** Separate the object. The June 2025 TIC survey puts official long-term Treasuries at **$3.500 trillion**, official short-term debt at **$405 billion**, official equities at **$2.230 trillion**, and mainland China’s Treasuries at **$657 billion** (equities **$344 billion**). **Fact.** [U.S. Treasury SB0482](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0482) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary.** Historically *observed* official sales — China’s 2014–16 reserve rundown (TIC Chinese Treasury holdings down about **$210 billion**) and the March–April 2020 official sales (about **$220 billion**) — did not produce a dollar-down equity crash. The 2015–16 10-year yield *fell* **11 basis points**; March 2020 was a risk-off in which the dollar was bid and the Fed backstops were used. **Fact.** [USCC staff paper](https://www.uscc.gov/sites/default/files/Research/U.S.%20Financial%20Exposure%20to%20China.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary.** [NY Fed Staff Report 983](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr983.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary.** Neely’s St. Louis Fed scenario for sale of China’s entire then-**$3 trillion** reserve portfolio is only **19–32 basis points** along the curve. **Fact (scenario).** [Neely, St. Louis Fed WP 2017-001](https://files.stlouisfed.org/files/htdocs/wp/2017/2017-001.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary.**

**Synthesis, not a new estimate.** For an observed-size official sale (low-hundreds of billions of Treasuries, or the **$429 billion** core-BRICS equity book), the combination of (i) the Fed earnings-translation coefficient, (ii) a **$65 trillion** domestic equity stock and a residual household *flow* near **$0.9 trillion** a year, (iii) FIMA/swaps if the motive is dollar liquidity, and (iv) a **$153 billion** stablecoin T-bill bid is large enough, on published stocks and coefficients, that a ≥20 percent S&P crash is not implied. For a *permanent* sale of the full official long-term Treasury book, FIMA is the wrong instrument, stablecoins are too small and too front-end, Mag7 earnings offset dollar translation rather than duration, and the household residual flow of one year covers only a fraction of **$3.5 trillion**. No Fed, BIS, or S&P DJI paper in this set tests that full-book counterfactual as an identified shock. The empirical sign of the dollar itself still cuts against the crash story: the two large modern U.S. bears (2008, 2022) arrived with a *stronger* dollar; the large modern dollar declines (Plaza, 2002–07, 2025 as a calendar year) arrived with *rising* or recovered S&P prices.

> Conflicts noted:
> - Single-sample vendor correlations disagree in both sign and size (2012–20 positive in one cycle note; 2016–23 daily −0.51; 2013–23 returns −0.098). The official episode record is the reliable object, not a pooled ρ.
> - 2022 S&P decline: S&P DJI price **−19.44 percent**, Damodaran total return **−18.04 percent**, Silverblatt June 13 print **−21.8 percent**, later vendor peak-to-trough about **−25 percent** (January 3 to October 12). All are ≥ the 20 percent bear screen; they are not the same number.
> - 2025 two-day drop: SF Fed **11 percent** (April 2–4) versus other houses’ **13 percent** / “as much as 15 percent” windows. Calendar 2025 was **+16–18 percent**.
> - 2020 swap-line peak: NY Fed EPR **$449 billion** versus Brookings **$470 billion**.
> - FIMA: “minimal” usage (NY Fed) versus a **$1.404 billion** peak (YPFS). Both are two orders of magnitude below the **$220 billion** official Treasury sales of March–April 2020.
> - S&P foreign-sales share: S&P DJI’s preferred 2018 print is **42.9 percent**; the unadjusted “all members” print is **28.3 percent**; FactSet’s 2022 working number is **40 percent**. No 2024–25 S&P DJI foreign-sales report was located on 2026-09-12.
> - Household equity *transactions* are a Z.1 residual and can change sign with other sectors’ measurement; they are not a survey of retail tickets.
>
> Gaps:
