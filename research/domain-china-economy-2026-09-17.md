## Macro Snapshot

### Growth and Official Targets

According to the National Bureau of Statistics (NBS), mainland China expanded **5.0 percent** year on year at constant prices in 2025, matching the “around 5 percent” target in the 2025 Government Work Report. Preliminary GDP was 140,187.9 billion yuan at current prices. Quarterly real growth slowed through the year: 5.4 percent in Q1, 5.2 percent in Q2, 4.8 percent in Q3, and 4.5 percent in Q4. Those figures are in the NBS year-end release of 19 January 2026 and the Statistical Communiqué of 28 February 2026. [NBS 2025 year-end release](https://www.stats.gov.cn/english/PressRelease/202601/t20260119_1962328.html) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High). **Primary.** Full-year 2024 real GDP also grew 5.0 percent. [NBS 2024 Statistical Communiqué](https://www.stats.gov.cn/english/PressRelease/202502/t20250228_1958822.html) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High). **Primary.**

The latest official quarterly print as of 17 September 2026 is Q2 2026. On 15 July the NBS reported first-half GDP of 69,570.4 billion yuan, up **4.7 percent** year on year. Q1 was up **5.0 percent**; Q2 was up **4.3 percent** year on year and 0.9 percent quarter on quarter. Reuters and CNBC reported the same prints and noted that 4.3 percent was the weakest year-on-year rate since Q4 2022. No official Q3 2026 GDP print had been released by 17 September. [NBS H1 2026 English release](https://www.stats.gov.cn/english/PressRelease/202607/t20260715_1964120.html) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High); [Reuters, 15 July 2026](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/chinas-q2-gdp-growth-slows-43-yy-misses-market-forecast-2026-07-15/) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High). **Primary / Established.**

Premier Li Qiang’s Government Work Report, delivered 5 March 2026, set the 2026 GDP target at **4.5–5 percent**, “while striving for better in practice.” That range is below the 2025 outturn and the 2025 “around 5 percent” target. Reuters described it as the lowest such target in decades of comparable reporting. The same report set surveyed urban unemployment around 5.5 percent, more than 12 million new urban jobs, and CPI around 2 percent. 2026 is the opening year of the 15th Five-Year Plan (2026–2030). [Xinhua/gov.cn NPC target](https://english.www.gov.cn/2026special/2026npcandcpcc/202603/05/content_WS69a8ea12c6d00ca5f9a0987b.html) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High); [Reuters, 5 March 2026](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/view-china-sets-2026-growth-target-455-below-last-years-pace-2026-03-05/) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High). **Primary / Established.**

NBS said first-half growth of 4.7 percent “meets the full-year expected growth target.” Reuters noted that 4.7 percent sits inside the band, which reduced urgency for a large stimulus even though Q2’s 4.3 percent fell below the floor and missed a Reuters poll of 4.5 percent. Simple arithmetic on the official numbers implies that second-half growth near 4.3 percent would still deliver about 4.5 percent for the year; a return toward 5 percent in H2 would be needed to hit the top of the range. That is an arithmetic implication, not an official H2 forecast. [gov.cn NBS press Q&A](https://www.gov.cn/lianbo/fabu/202607/content_7075948.htm) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High). **Primary.**

International forecasts cluster around **4.4–4.6 percent for 2026** and **4.1–4.5 percent for 2027**, and most were closed before the 15 July Q2 print. The IMF July 2026 WEO Update projects 4.6 percent in 2026 and 4.1 percent in 2027, after 5.0 percent in 2024 and 2025. The World Bank’s July *China Economic Update* (data cutoff 30 June) forecasts 4.4 / 4.3 / 4.2 percent for 2026–28. The OECD June Outlook projects 4.5 and 4.3 percent. The ADB left its forecast unchanged in July at 4.6 and 4.5 percent. [IMF WEO Update July 2026 PDF](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/weo/2026/update/july/english/text.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High); [World Bank CEU PDF](https://thedocs.worldbank.org/en/doc/0cb2fc6dd88d4db3816dc0433b5cb49b-0070012026/original/CEU-July-2026-EN.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High); [OECD China chapter](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/oecd-economic-outlook-volume-2026-issue-1_2d1956f0-en/full-report/china_6526c66b.html) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High); [ADB July 2026 news](https://www.adb.org/news/adb-sees-slower-growth-asia-and-pacific-2026-amid-global-energy-crisis) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High). **Primary.**

Private forecasts moved after the Q2 miss. Reuters reported Morgan Stanley cut 2026 to **4.6 percent from 4.8 percent**. UBS’s published baseline is 4.5 percent in 2026 and 4.6 percent in 2027. Citi’s mid-year outlook held 4.7 percent. A mid-September secondary report that Goldman Sachs cut 2026 to 4.5 percent from 4.6 percent was not located on Goldman’s public site. [Reuters on MS cut after Q2](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/chinas-q2-gdp-growth-slows-43-yy-misses-market-forecast-2026-07-15/) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High); [UBS China Outlook](https://www.ubs.com/global/en/investment-bank/insights-and-data/articles/china-outlook.html) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: Medium). **Established.**

On the expenditure side, the 2025 Statistical Communiqué states that final consumption contributed **2.6 percentage points** to the 5.0 percent real growth rate, gross capital formation **0.8 points**, and net exports **1.6 points**. In H1 2026, NBS official Zheng Xuegong wrote that consumption pulled growth by **2.1 points**, GCF by **1.7 points**, and net exports by **0.8 points**. Monthly fixed-asset investment (FAI) is a different series: it fell 5.7 percent in H1 and **7.2 percent** in January–August, with real-estate development investment down **19.9 percent**. NBS’s August English statement, as quoted by CNBC, warned of an “acute” imbalance between strong supply and weak demand. This suggests the official GCF contribution is hard to square with collapsing FAI—the discrepancy independent estimators highlight. [NBS Statistical Communiqué 2025](https://www.stats.gov.cn/english/PressRelease/202602/t20260228_1962661.html) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High); [Zheng Xuegong on H1 2026](https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/zxfbhjd/202607/t20260716_1964139.html) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High); [CNBC on August data, 15 September 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/15/china-august-retail-sales-industrial-output-investment-exports-.html) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High). **Primary / Established.**

Controversy over data quality is explicit. Rhodium Group, in a 22 December 2025 note written before the official 5.0 percent print, estimated 2025 real growth at **2.5–3.0 percent** and projected **1.0–2.5 percent** for 2026, with most of the gap in investment. The Bank of Finland’s BOFIT estimated 2025 growth about one percentage point below the official 5.0 percent, and after the Q2 print put alternative growth at **3.2 percent** in Q1 (versus official 5.0) and **3.1 percent** in Q2 (versus official 4.3). BOFIT’s forecast expected “actual” growth around 4 percent in 2026. NBS has not published a rebuttal figure. [Rhodium, 22 December 2025](https://rhg.com/research/chinas-economy-rightsizing-2025-looking-ahead-to-2026/) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: Medium as a single research house); [BOFIT Weekly 30/2026](https://www.bofit.fi/en/monitoring/weekly/2026/vw202630_1/) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: Medium). **Established (independent research).**

The 30 July 2026 Politburo meeting, after the Q2 miss, pledged to accelerate already-budgeted fiscal spending and to roll out “pragmatic and effective incremental” measures. Reuters and ING read the statement as rejecting a large new stimulus package. [Xinhua Politburo readout via china.org.cn](http://www.china.org.cn/china/Off_the_Wire/2026-07/30/content_118626302.shtml) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High); [Reuters on Politburo, 30 July 2026](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/chinas-top-leaders-pledge-incremental-measures-support-economy-2026-07-30/) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High). **Primary / Established.**

Whether China is “on track” depends on which series one uses. On official NBS real GDP, H1 2026 growth of 4.7 percent is inside the 4.5–5 percent band, but Q2’s 4.3 percent is below it and August consumption and investment are consistent with a soft Q3. On Rhodium and BOFIT alternatives, the economy is not on track for 4.5–5 percent in any meaningful sense.

> Conflicts noted: Official NBS 2025 growth of 5.0% vs Rhodium 2.5–3.0% and BOFIT ~4%. Official Q2 2026 of 4.3% vs BOFIT alternative 3.1%. NBS H1 GCF contribution of +1.7 points vs FAI −5.7% (and −7.2% through August). IMF/WB/OECD/ADB 2026 forecasts of 4.4–4.6% were largely closed before the Q2 miss.
>
> Gaps: No official Q3 2026 GDP as of 2026-09-17. No NBS split of GCF into fixed capital versus inventories. No Rhodium update after December 2025. No IMF or World Bank forecast revision after the Q2 print located.

### Prices and Deflation

As of 17 September 2026, mainland China is **not** in headline consumer-price deflation. According to NBS, August CPI rose **0.8 percent** year on year and **0.4 percent** month on month, after July’s 0.5 percent year-on-year increase. The January–August average was **0.9 percent**. Food was still down 1.4 percent year on year; energy jumped to 4.1 percent and, according to NBS’s Dong Lijuan, contributed about 0.28 points to the headline. Core CPI excluding food and energy rose **1.0 percent**. These are not seasonally adjusted. Starting January 2026, CPI uses a 2025 base; NBS says the rotation changed monthly year-on-year CPI by about 0.06 points on average. September 2026 CPI had not been released. [NBS, CPI August 2026](https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/zxfbhjd/202609/t20260909_1965263.html) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High); [Reuters, 9 Sep 2026](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/chinas-producer-inflation-regains-momentum-august-2026-09-09/) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High). **Primary / Established.**

The 2026 CPI path is a mild, uneven lift from the 2023–25 near-zero episode, not a return to the official ~2 percent target. Monthly year-on-year CPI ran 0.2, 1.3 (Lunar New Year), 1.0, 1.2, 1.2, 1.0, 0.5, then 0.8 percent from January through August. Full-year 2024 CPI was **+0.2 percent**; 2025 was **0.0 percent**. [NBS 2025 statistical communiqué](https://www.stats.gov.cn/english/PressRelease/202602/t20260228_1962661.html) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High); [PBOC Q2 2026 Monetary Policy Report](https://www.pbc.gov.cn/goutongjiaoliu/113456/113469/2026081218034520348/index.html) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High). **Primary.**

Factory-gate prices tell a sharper exit from a long deflation. August PPI rose **3.8 percent** year on year. PPI had been negative for **41 consecutive months** from October 2022 through February 2026, then turned positive in March 2026 at +0.5 percent and printed 2.8, 3.9, 4.1, 3.5 and 3.8 percent in April–August. The rebound was concentrated in means of production (+5.0 percent), especially coal, non-ferrous metals, oil and chemicals. Consumer-goods PPI was still **−0.5 percent**. This suggests 2026 PPI reflation is real at the headline but still **cost-push and upstream**, not a restoration of downstream pricing power. [NBS, PPI August 2026 (English)](https://www.stats.gov.cn/english/PressRelease/202609/t20260910_1965274.html) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High); [Xinhua via People’s Daily, 10 Apr 2026](https://en.people.cn/n3/2026/0410/c90000-20445362.html) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High). **Primary.**

The GDP deflator, which NBS does not publish as a named series, is inferred as nominal minus real growth. In Q2 2026, Caixin and others report real GDP +4.3 percent and nominal +5.9 percent, implying a deflator of about **+1.6 percent** and ending **12 consecutive negative quarters** since Q1 2023. The Q2 turn is attributed to AI-related prices and oil shocks, not a consumption boom. [Caixin, 15 Jul 2026](https://www.caixinglobal.com/2026-07-15/chinas-growth-misses-but-ai-and-oil-prices-snap-its-3-year-deflation-streak-102464387.html) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High); [IMF 2025 Article IV, CR 26/044](https://www.imf.org/en/-/media/files/publications/cr/2026/english/1chnea2026001-source-pdf.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High). **Established / Primary.**

Housing in the CPI basket was **−0.3 percent** year on year in August; ING’s Lynn Song, cited by Reuters, notes rent at **−0.6 percent** for a fifth consecutive month. New-home prices in most of the 70-city sample were still falling (see Property Market). Capital Economics, quoted by Reuters and CNBC, argues both CPI and PPI will ease if Gulf energy flows normalize; Danske Bank in early September cut its 2026 CPI forecast to **0.8 percent** from 1.0 percent. The IMF’s January Article IV projected 2026 average CPI of only 0.9 percent and a still-negative GDP deflator; year-to-August CPI of 0.9 percent is close to that CPI number, but PPI and the Q2 deflator have already overshot the January baseline. [CNBC, 9 Sep 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/09/china-cpi-ppi-august-oil-prices-tech-manufacturing-.html) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High). **Established.**

With the one-year LPR at 3.0 percent and August CPI at 0.8 percent, the simple ex-post real one-year LPR is about **2.2 percentage points**—lower than in 2025, when CPI averaged 0.0 percent against the same LPR, but still high relative to a ~1 percent inflation regime. Verdict in one sentence: China in September 2026 is in **low positive inflation at the consumer level**, after exiting a 41-month PPI decline in March and a 12-quarter negative GDP deflator in Q2; the rebound is energy-, commodity- and tech-cost driven, with food, rents, consumer-goods PPI and home prices still falling.

> Conflicts noted: Official “mild rebound” language versus market economists who treat August as a cost-push bounce. Headline PPI +3.8% versus consumer-goods PPI −0.5% and PBOC CGPI −1.1% in H1. IMF January 2026 deflator forecast of −0.7% versus Q2 derived deflator of about +1.6%.
>
> Gaps: No September 2026 CPI/PPI. No official NBS GDP-deflator series. No 2026 monthly official wage-inflation index.

### Labor Market and Demographics

Mainland China’s headline labour market in 2026 has stayed inside the government’s target band, while youth unemployment (excluding students) followed the usual July graduate-season spike and the working-age population continued to shrink. NBS reported that the urban surveyed unemployment rate averaged **5.2 percent in January–August 2026**, unchanged from a year earlier, and stood at **5.3 percent in August**. The 2026 official target is around 5.5 percent unemployment and more than 12 million new urban jobs. Prime-age (30–59) unemployment was **3.9 percent** in August. [NBS English August release](https://www.stats.gov.cn/english/PressRelease/202609/t20260915_1965305.html) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High). **Primary.**

Youth unemployment is still published, but **not on the old 2018–June 2023 definition**. NBS suspended the inclusive 16–24 series after it reached 21.3 percent in June 2023. From December 2023 it resumed a revised series that **excludes students**. The latest confirmed print is **July 2026: 17.9 percent** for ages 16–24 excluding students, up from 14.9 percent in June and the highest since August 2025. Reuters, Yicai and SCMP match on these figures. An August 2026 age-group print was not in the 15 September monthly release. [Reuters, 19 Aug 2026](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/chinas-youth-jobless-rate-hits-11-month-high-july-2026-08-19/) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High); [NBS methodology note, 17 Jan 2024](https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/zxfb/202401/t20240117_1946641.html) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High). **Established / Primary.**

College-graduate supply is at a record: about **12.7 million** in 2026, versus about 12.22 million in 2025. H1 2026 urban new jobs were **6.95 million**, unchanged year on year. Full-year 2025 urban new jobs were 12.67 million. Total employment in the annual communiqués **fell**: 734.39 million at end-2024 to **725.04 million** at end-2025. [CGTN / Wang Xiaoping, 7 Mar 2026](https://news.cgtn.com/news/2026-03-07/China-s-college-graduates-to-reach-12-7-million-in-2026-official-1LjxAwzhRGo/p.html) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High); [NBS 2025 Statistical Communiqué](https://www.stats.gov.cn/english/PressRelease/202602/t20260228_1962661.html) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High). **Primary.**

Migrant-worker numbers ticked up and wages rose slowly. The 2025 NBS survey put the total at **301.15 million** (+0.5 percent); average monthly income was **5,075 yuan (+2.3 percent)**. In 2025, official labour productivity (+6.1 percent) outpaced urban unit wage growth (about 3–4 percent). Part of the productivity gain is mechanical: the employment stock fell by about 9.35 million while real GDP rose 5.0 percent. [NBS 2025 migrant-worker report](https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/zxfb/202604/t20260430_1963472.html) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High). **Primary.**

Population continued to shrink, and the 2025 birth drop was larger than 2022–2023. Year-end 2025 population was **1,404.89 million (−3.39 million)**; births **7.92 million** (CBR 5.63 per thousand) after a Dragon-year rebound to 9.54 million in 2024. Ages 16–59 were **851.36 million (60.6 percent)**; 65+ were **223.65 million (15.9 percent)**. Delayed retirement took effect 1 January 2025 (men 60→63 over 15 years). A national childcare subsidy of **3,600 yuan per child per year** until age three began the same day; 2025 births were nonetheless the lowest official total of the decade so far. That is not proof the subsidy failed—2025 births largely reflect conceptions before nationwide rollout. [NBS 2025 Communiqué](https://www.stats.gov.cn/zwfwck/sjfb/202602/t20260228_1962662.html) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High); [SCIO / China Daily, 15 Sept 2026](http://english.scio.gov.cn/m/pressroom/2026-09/15/content_118696242.html) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High). **Primary.**

UN *World Population Prospects 2024* still sits above NBS’s end-2025 total; use NBS for recent levels. Permanent urbanization reached **67.89 percent** at end-2025; the latest national hukou-urbanization rate found is **48.3 percent in 2023**.

> Conflicts noted: UN WPP 2024 vs NBS levels. ADB assigned 12.7 million graduates to 2025; Chinese official sources assign that figure to 2026. August 16–24 18.9% is the **August 2025** print, not August 2026.
>
> Gaps: August 2026 16–24 excluding-students rate. Inclusive 16–24 series still unpublished. 2026 full-year births. Official evaluation of delayed-retirement labour-market effects.

## Demand, Property, and Industry

### Household Consumption

Official retail sales remain weak through mid-September 2026 and are still reported in **nominal** terms. NBS stated that August total retail sales of consumer goods were 3,982.4 billion yuan, up **0.4 percent** year on year, a slowdown from 0.6 percent in July versus a 0.8 percent Reuters-poll forecast. January–August sales rose **1.1 percent**. Excluding automobiles, August sales rose 2.5 percent. These growth rates are **not adjusted for prices**. Full-year 2025 retail sales were +3.7 percent nominal. The 2026 monthly path decelerated almost every month: +2.8 percent in January–February down to +0.4 percent in August. [NBS via gov.cn](https://english.www.gov.cn/archive/statistics/202609/15/content_WS6aa8ac5ec6d00ca5f9a0d2f5.html) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High); [Reuters](https://www.reuters.com/world/china/chinas-factory-output-growth-quickens-in-august-retail-sales-slow-2026-09-15/) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High). **Primary / Established.**

NBS does not publish an official monthly real retail-sales series. CPI rose 0.8 percent in August and 0.9 percent on average in January–August. A crude CPI subtraction would make August real retail slightly negative and January–August real growth near zero. That is a rough deflator, not an official real rate.

Services are outpacing goods. In January–August, service retail rose **4.9 percent** and goods retail **1.0 percent**. August category data: automobiles **−18.5 percent**; furniture **−7.9 percent**; building materials **−11.8 percent**; jewelry **−17.5 percent**; household appliances **+2.3 percent** (first positive month since September 2025); communication equipment **+27.3 percent**. [NBS table via Sina](https://finance.sina.com.cn/money/future/indu/2026-09-15/doc-inirwmwe3727681.shtml) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High). **Primary.**

Household survey spending is lagging income. In H1 2026, per capita disposable income rose **5.2 percent nominal / 4.2 percent real**; consumption expenditure rose **3.7 percent nominal / 2.7 percent real**. Property income grew only 1.1 percent. The IMF’s 2025 Article IV states that the household saving rate remained elevated and above pre-pandemic levels. Official consumer confidence remains in the high-80s to low-90s (June 2026 composite **89.4** versus 100 as neutrality). [NBS H1 2026 income](https://www.stats.gov.cn/english/PressRelease/202607/t20260717_1964154.html) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High); [IMF Country Report 26/044](https://www.imf.org/en/-/media/files/publications/cr/2026/english/1chnea2026001-source-pdf.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High). **Primary.**

The trade-in (以旧换新) program was renewed for 2026 with **CNY 250 billion** of ultra-long special treasury-bond funding, down from CNY 300 billion in 2025. MOFCOM: H1 2026 related sales **1.1 trillion yuan**, about one-third lower than H1 2025’s 1.6 trillion. By late August, 187.5 billion yuan of funds had been allocated, generating about 1.32 trillion yuan of related sales. IMF staff described the 2025 appliance trade-in as about **0.1 percent of GDP**. This is a fading cyclical stimulus, not a new consumption engine. [ADB April 2026 PDF](https://www.adb.org/sites/default/files/publication/1135881/prc-ado-april-2026.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High); [Caixin](https://companies.caixin.com/m/2026-07-23/102467359.html) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High). **Established.**

CAAM: January–August 2026 vehicle sales **20.315 million, −3.8 percent**. NEV share is still rising (August 60.6 percent monthly). Just Auto, citing CAAM, reports January–August **domestic** sales **−22 percent** and exports **+67 percent**. NBS auto *retail* (−18.5 percent in August) is a different series from CAAM wholesale including exports. [Gasgoo citing CAAM](https://autonews.gasgoo.com/articles/news/caam-auto-production-and-sales-rebound-month-on-month-in-august-monthly-nev-share-hits-new-high-2098629863337791489) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High). **Established.**

Housing remains the household balance-sheet constraint. IMF: housing investment fell from 12.3 percent of GDP in 2020 to **6.1 percent in 2025**. Rogoff and Yang (Brookings, 2026) estimate that a 10 percent house-price decline cuts consumption by about 1.5–2.3 percent. Furniture and building-materials retail remain in contraction. Tourism volume is still expanding (H1 domestic trips +5.4 percent) but spending grew only +2.0 percent, so per-trip outlays are falling. [IMF CR 26/044](https://www.imf.org/en/-/media/files/publications/cr/2026/english/1chnea2026001-source-pdf.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High); [China Daily citing MCT](https://www.chinadaily.com.cn/a/202608/07/WS6a75cec3a310986e2b469a11.html) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High). **Primary.**

NBS: household consumption was **40.0 percent of GDP in 2025** (from 37.9 percent in 2020). Final consumption contributed 52.0 percent of 2025 GDP *growth* but only about 46 percent of H1 2026 growth. Through August 2026 there is **no broad household-consumption rebound**.

> Conflicts noted: NBS appliances +2.3% in August vs a Trading Economics −1.1% reprint (treat +2.3% as Primary). CAAM wholesale vs NBS auto retail are different coverage. Household deposits rose less in 2026 than in 2025; that is not the same as a lower saving rate.
>
> Gaps: No official real monthly retail-sales growth. No SNA household saving rate for 2025–26. No 2026 PBOC depositor-survey “more saving” share located.

### Property Market

As of 15 September 2026, the NBS 70-city survey shows a market that is no longer falling as fast in prices, especially in first-tier cities, but is still declining in most cities and in the bulk of construction, sales, and investment. ## External and Structural Forces

### Geopolitics, Tariffs, and FDI

As of 17 September 2026, the operative US–China commercial status quo is a one-year Busan/Kuala Lumpur truce, not a new comprehensive deal. After the 30 October 2025 Trump–Xi meeting, the White House said the United States would cut the fentanyl-related additional duty from 20 to 10 percent effective 10 November 2025, keep a 10 percent “reciprocal” additional duty, and suspend “heightened” reciprocal tariffs until 10 November 2026. Reuters contemporaneously put the post-Busan average US duty on Chinese goods at about **47 percent**, down from about 57 percent. That IEEPA-based stack did not survive 2026 intact: on 20 February 2026 the US Supreme Court held that IEEPA does not authorize presidential tariffs. Subsequent legal layers (new Section 301/232 work, a 12.5 percent assignment in a later forced-labor partner action) mean there is **no single verified current average US tariff on China**. [White House Executive Order, 4 Nov 2025](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2025/11/modifying-reciprocal-tariff-rates-consistent-with-the-economic-and-trade-arrangement-between-the-united-states-and-the-peoples-republic-of-china/) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High); [Reuters, 1 Nov 2025](https://www.reuters.com/world/us/what-did-trump-xi-agree-tariffs-export-controls-fentanyl-2025-11-01/) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High); [Reuters, 20 Feb 2026](https://www.reuters.com/legal/government/us-supreme-court-rejects-trumps-global-tariffs-2026-02-20/) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High). **Primary / Established.**

The Reuters **$30 billion** reciprocal-cut story is real, but it is a consultation, not a signed cut. On 10 September 2026 MOFCOM said the two economic teams were consulting on a reciprocal tariff-reduction framework covering $30 billion of products from each side. ### Industrial Policy

On 12 March 2026 the NPC approved the Outline of the 15th Five-Year Plan (2026–2030). It puts manufacturing at the centre: “keep a reasonable share of manufacturing,” pursue “new quality productive forces,” and seek “decisive breakthroughs” in integrated circuits, industrial mother machines, basic software, advanced materials and biomanufacturing. AI is both a sector and a horizontal “AI+” campaign. GDP is not given as a five-year average; annual targets are to be set as circumstances require, in service of doubling 2020 per-capita GDP by 2035. The same chapter lists raising the household consumption rate alongside supply-side upgrading. [15th FYP Outline, gov.cn](https://www.gov.cn/yaowen/liebiao/202603/content_7062633.htm) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High). **Primary.**

NDRC chairman Zheng Shanjie said six major emerging pillar industries, including intelligent robots, are expected to exceed **10 trillion yuan** of combined output by 2030; core digital-economy industries are targeted at **12.5 percent of GDP**. MIIT’s Xin Guobin said manufacturing value-added rose from 26.5 trillion yuan in 2020 to **34.7 trillion**, remaining around **25 percent of GDP**. The Plan requires a standing capacity-monitoring mechanism to “comprehensively rectify ‘involution-style’ competition.” [MIIT / SCIO briefing](https://hca.miit.gov.cn/gzcy/zbft/art/2026/art_917c27e514ec4c6283c58d5ec57ca11d.html) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High). **Primary.**

Semiconductor signals are the clearest “upgrading succeeding” evidence. CXMT listed on STAR on 27 July 2026 after raising about **57.92 billion yuan**—Asia’s largest IPO of 2026. Reuters, citing the prospectus, reported CXMT as the world’s fourth-largest DRAM maker with about 7.7 percent 2025 share. SMIC’s Q2 2026 revenue was **$3.01 billion** (+36.1 percent year on year); Reuters reported utilization **93.7 percent** and that SMIC is “the only Chinese foundry able to mass-produce logic chips such as CPUs and GPUs on a 7-nanometre process.” EUV remains blocked; HBM3E mass production is unconfirmed. [Reuters CXMT](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/what-is-cxmt-how-did-it-become-chinas-dram-champion-2026-07-27/) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High); [SMIC HKEX filing](https://www1.hkexnews.hk/listedco/listconews/sehk/2026/0813/2026081300353.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High). **Primary / Established.**

NEV sales rose from 1.367 million to **16.49 million** over the 14th FYP; China accounts for more than 70 percent of global NEV, power-battery and key-materials output. H1 2026 NEV exports were **2.35 million (+120 percent)**. Auto manufacturing capacity utilization was about **70.6 percent** in H1 2026. On 9 September 2026, MIIT and eight departments issued a 15th FYP NEV plan with 2030 targets of 70 percent of new passenger-car sales and, for the first time, standalone “capacity early-warning and regulation.” Caixin reported a temporary pause on new battery-project construction pending a year-end review. NEV purchase-tax exemption steps down to a 50 percent reduction in 2026–2027. [MIIT / SCIO](https://hca.miit.gov.cn/gzcy/zbft/art/2026/art_917c27e514ec4c6283c58d5ec57ca11d.html) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High); [CnEVPost](https://cnevpost.com/2026/09/11/china-unveils-five-year-nev-plan/) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High). **Primary / Established.**

No official nationwide 2026 closure total for NEVs, batteries or solar was found. Anti-involution tools are overwhelmingly *new-capacity gates*, replacement ratios, and price/cost policing, not published realized shutdowns. The NY Fed found China’s solar-panel export *revenues* fell from $46 billion in 2022 to **$28 billion in 2025** because prices collapsed even as volumes stayed dominant. On 11 March 2026 USTR self-initiated Section 301 investigations into “structural excess capacity” covering China and 15 other economies, citing China’s 2025 global goods surplus above **$1.2 trillion**. MOFCOM’s July 2026 position paper argued 2025 industrial utilization was **74.4 percent** and that Western measures are protectionism. Both can be true: the average is pulled up by electronics, while autos, batteries and PV sit at or below commonly cited healthy lines and leak into foreign markets. [USTR](https://ustr.gov/about/policy-offices/press-office/press-releases/2026/march/ustr-initiates-section-301-investigations-relating-structural-excess-capacity-and-production) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High); [MOFCOM Chinese text](http://www.mofcom.gov.cn/zwjg/jmxw/yz/art/2026/art_c29762e2ad0f496397571675311acbad.html) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High); [NY Fed Liberty Street](https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2026/03/chinas-electric-trade/) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High). **Primary.**

This suggests industrial policy is succeeding at building “new quality productive forces” and exportable green-tech scale; it has not, on 2026 evidence, rebalanced the growth model or produced a quantified capacity purge comparable to 2016 steel.

### Regulatory Backdrop

China’s first dedicated private-economy statute, the Private Economy Promotion Law, was adopted 30 April 2025 and took effect **20 May 2025** (78 articles). It writes the “two unswervings” into statute and defines private organizations as PRC-established entities controlled by Chinese citizens. The political “confidence” campaign that preceded it was Xi Jinping’s 17 February 2025 private-enterprise symposium. The 2023 “31-point plan” remains the parent Party-state policy; 2025–26 implementation is now statutory plus departmental work plans. [Supreme People’s Court / Xinhua English](https://english.court.gov.cn/2025-05/07/c_1090787.htm) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High); [gov.cn 31-point opinions](https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/202307/content_6893055.htm) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High). **Primary.**

The regulatory pendulum has swung toward *supporting* private firms in law and slogans, but not toward *deregulating* them. The 2025 Market Access Negative List cut items from 117 to **106**; NDRC itself said “glass door / spring door / revolving door” barriers outside the list had not been eliminated. NBS data show private fixed-asset investment down **10.1 percent** year on year in January–August 2026 (−6.4 percent excluding real-estate development). Qiushi in September 2026 still listed hidden access barriers, financing difficulty, and excessive inspections. [NDRC Q&A](https://www.ndrc.gov.cn/xxgk/jd/jd/202504/t20250424_1397372.html) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High); [SCIO / Xinhua, NBS](http://english.scio.gov.cn/pressroom/2026-09/15/content_118696876.html) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High). **Primary.**

The 2020–23 platform crackdown’s 2026 end-state is “normalize and legalize.” SAMR fined Trip.com Group a total of **RMB 5.179 billion** on 25 July 2026 for abuse of dominance in online hotel booking, presented as continuity from Alibaba and Meituan “choose-one-of-two” cases. New 2026 rules cover platform pricing, private rules, and SME access to platform resources. Data-security implementing rules (outbound personal-information certification from 1 January 2026; network-data risk assessment from 20 August 2026) tighten process rather than repeal the 2021–24 stack. [SAMR / *Economic Daily*](https://www.samr.gov.cn/xw/mtjj/art/2026/art_65a35a1016e84e3f996bae4d07736e4d.html) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High); [CAC](https://www.cac.gov.cn/2025-10/17/c_1762449728720008.htm) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High). **Primary.**

The State Council’s 22 May 2026 opinions on providing basic public services at the place of permanent residence aim to “gradually eliminate factors linking basic public services to household registration.” This is residence-based equalization, **not abolition of hukou**, with “one city, one policy” and megacity caveats. The 28 August property sales-system notice prefers completed-home sales without abolishing presales. Together they are institutional substitutes for the 2010s growth machine (migrant labor without full urban entitlements; presale-and-leverage property). Whether that restores private *investment* is not yet shown in the 2026 NBS numbers. [gov.cn full text](https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/content/202605/content_7069960.htm) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High); [Xinhua Q&A on 8·28](https://www.xinhuanet.com/politics/20260828/ce3eb65e9e92410f8f9e4e81a6a92da3/c.html) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High). **Primary.**

> Conflicts noted: Count of “supporting measures” after the private-economy law is unsettled (53 / 150+ / 170+). Official confidence metrics sit beside private FAI of −10.1%. SCMP described 8·28 as shifting “away from a presales model”; the official notice keeps a dual track.
>
> Gaps: No independent audit of hukou-reform take-up (opinions are four months old). Quantitative effect of 8·28 on starts and land sales is too early to measure.

## Key Findings

China in mid-September 2026 is a **two-speed economy** on the official numbers, and a slower economy still if one uses independent estimators. According to NBS, H1 2026 real GDP grew **4.7 percent**, inside the NPC’s **4.5–5 percent** target, but Q2 slowed to **4.3 percent**—the weakest year-on-year print since Q4 2022—and August retail sales (+0.4 percent) and fixed-asset investment (−7.2 percent year to date) are consistent with a soft third quarter. [NBS H1 2026](https://www.stats.gov.cn/english/PressRelease/202607/t20260715_1964120.html) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High); [CNBC, 15 September 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/15/china-august-retail-sales-industrial-output-investment-exports-.html) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High). IMF, World Bank, OECD and ADB forecasts that start from official accounts cluster at **4.4–4.6 percent** for 2026. Rhodium’s December 2025 alternative of **1.0–2.5 percent** for 2026, and BOFIT’s post-Q2 alternative of about **3.1 percent** in Q2 versus official 4.3 percent, are not a rounding error; they are a different reading of investment. This report treats official NBS as the policy-relevant scoreboard and flags the independent gap as a **High-importance, Medium-confidence** data-quality risk rather than silently picking one number.

The second finding is that **the 2023–25 consumer-price deflation episode has ended at the headline, without a demand-led reflation**. August CPI was +0.8 percent year on year; PPI turned positive in March after 41 negative months and printed +3.8 percent in August; the derived GDP deflator turned positive in Q2 after 12 negative quarters. [NBS CPI August](https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/zxfbhjd/202609/t20260909_1965263.html) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High); [Caixin, 15 Jul 2026](https://www.caixinglobal.com/2026-07-15/chinas-growth-misses-but-ai-and-oil-prices-snap-its-3-year-deflation-streak-102464387.html) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High). Food, rents, consumer-goods PPI and home prices were still falling. The rebound is energy, commodities and AI-linked electronics. Real policy rates have eased mechanically as CPI left zero, but the 1-year LPR has not been cut in 2026 and still sits about 2.2 points above August CPI. Households are not behaving as if inflation has returned: H1 real consumption grew more slowly than real income, consumer confidence remains below 90, and auto retail was −18.5 percent in August.

The third finding is that **property is a split bottom, not a sector bottom**, and it still binds local finance, household wealth and credit. First-tier new-home prices ticked up month-on-month in August and existing-home *volumes* rose 10.6 percent year to date, but new-home sales, investment (−19.9 percent), starts (−24.8 percent) and developer funds are contracting *faster* than in 2025 as a whole. Land-sale revenue was **−30.8 percent** in January–July. [NBS January–August 2026 real estate](https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/zxfb/202609/t20260915_1965310.html) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High); [MOF H1 2026](https://www.mof.gov.cn/zhengwuxinxi/caizhengxinwen/202607/t20260722_3994019.htm) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High). The 28 August completed-home-sales reform is an attempt to exit the presale model; it tightens developer working capital before it restores private demand. Distress has migrated from 2023–24 private defaults to state-backed Vanke’s restricted-default exchanges. Pan Gongsheng’s *Qiushi* statement that property and LGFV loans are declining, and that filling that hole is a precondition for total credit to rise, is the cleanest official admission that the old credit engine is in reverse. [Pan Gongsheng, *Qiushi*](https://www.qstheory.cn/20260915/1c41fc49b4b44db4b2550ddd41f40315/c.html) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High).

The fourth finding is that **exports and high-tech manufacturing are carrying official growth, and that carry is politically and commercially fragile**. August dollar exports jumped 25 percent; the January–August goods surplus was $805.5 billion, on track to exceed $1 trillion for a second year. IC export *values* more than doubled while *volumes* rose about 4 percent. NEV and battery exports are booming even as domestic auto retail and capacity utilization sag. [Reuters, 8 Sep 2026](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/chinas-exports-up-25-yy-august-imports-surge-282-2026-09-08/) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High). Utilized FDI is still falling. US goods imports from China dropped sharply in 2025 while Vietnam’s surged, and Fed research shows Chinese firms inside that diversion. A US–China truce is holding as a pause, not a settlement; $30 billion reciprocal cuts were still talks as of 17 September; USTR has opened a new Section 301 on “structural excess capacity.” Industrial policy is succeeding at chips (CXMT IPO, SMIC utilization 93.7 percent) and green-tech scale; it is not succeeding at rebalancing toward household consumption. MOFCOM’s 74.4 percent average utilization and USTR’s $1.2 trillion surplus statistic can both be true.

The fifth finding is that **macro policy in 2026 is incremental by design**. The budget repeats 2025’s 4 percent headline deficit and 4.4 trillion yuan special-bond quota; the PBOC has not cut the 7-day rate or LPR in 2026; the July Politburo pledged to spend what is already budgeted. [MOF 2026 budget](https://www.mof.gov.cn/zhengwuxinxi/caizhengxinwen/202603/t20260316_3985331.htm) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High); [Reuters, 30 Jul 2026](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/chinas-top-leaders-pledge-incremental-measures-support-economy-2026-07-30/) (accessed 2026-09-17, confidence: High). Debt-swap progress on the *narrow* MOF hidden-debt stock looks real; the IMF’s augmented public deficit near 14 percent of GDP and LGFV perimeter several times the MOF stock remain the relevant solvency picture for local platforms. Markets agree with the incremental stance: 10-year CGBs near **1.69 percent**, A-shares slightly negative year to date after a 2025 rally, and a July equity floor that required an explicit CSRC/SOE put. Demographics tighten the medium-term constraint: 2025 births fell to 7.92 million, the working-age population shrank by 6.6 million, and July youth unemployment excluding students was 17.9 percent.

## Strategic Recommendations

1. **Treat official 4.5 percent as a ceiling, not a floor, for planning through 2027 — unless Beijing shifts from incremental to household-side fiscal expansion.** Rationale: H1 official growth is inside the band only because Q1 was 5.0 percent; Q2 was already below 4.5 percent, August demand is weaker, and most official-data-based forecasts sit at or just below the floor. Independent estimators are lower still. Evidence: NBS Q2 4.3 percent; IMF/WB/OECD/ADB 4.4–4.6 percent; August retail +0.4 percent; Politburo “incremental” language. [NBS](https://www.stats.gov.cn/english/PressRelease/202607/t20260715_1964120.html); [IMF WEO Update July 2026](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/weo/2026/update/july/english/text.pdf).

2. **Do not read headline PPI/CPI as a consumer recovery.** Rationale: the 2026 price rebound is upstream and imported. Downstream consumer-goods PPI, food, rents and home prices are still falling; real household consumption is lagging income. Evidence: NBS August CPI/PPI split; NBS H1 income vs consumption; Capital Economics/Danske via Reuters/CNBC. If Gulf energy normalizes, both CPI and PPI can fade again.

3. **Underwrite China exposure as industrial-upgrade plus property-overhang, not as a synchronized cycle.** Rationale: SMIC, CXMT, NEV/battery exports and high-tech manufacturing are a different economy from 70-city housing, land sales, private FAI (−10.1 percent) and LGFV balance sheets. Evidence: NBS industrial vs FAI/property prints; Fitch Vanke RD; MOF land sales −30.8 percent; MIIT 15th FYP industrial list. Portfolio and supply-chain decisions that average these two Chinas will be wrong on both.

4. **Watch the 24 September 2026 US visit and the November 2026 truce expiry as discrete event risk, not as background noise.** Rationale: the current commercial truce is a pause with disputed legal underpinnings after the IEEPA ruling; $30 billion reciprocal cuts were still consultation; utilized FDI is still negative; export values are inflated by AI prices. Evidence: White House Nov 2025 EO; Reuters 15 Sep 2026; MOFCOM FDI prints; Caixin on AI price-versus-volume in August exports. A failed visit or a lapsed truce would hit the one engine that is working.

5. **Credit the 15th FYP hukou-services and completed-home-sales reforms as the right *direction* and a 2026 *non-event* for demand.** Rationale: both attack the 2010s growth machine’s institutional core, which is what IMF staff say is required to lift consumption by several points of GDP. Implementation is gradient, local, and underfunded relative to the land-sale hole. Evidence: State Council May 2026 opinions; IMF WP/25/259 and Article IV; NBS retail path through August. This is a 2027–30 story if funded; it is not a 2026 retail-sales catalyst.
