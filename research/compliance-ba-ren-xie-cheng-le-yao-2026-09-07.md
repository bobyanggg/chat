# Implementation Considerations — Security and Compliance

> ⚠️ This section surfaces publicly available legal information for research purposes only. It does not constitute legal advice.

This axis covers six public-information questions about the August 2026 Bilibili short film commonly called 《把人写成了妖》 (full title 《我这一生最大的罪，是把人写成了妖……》): China’s 2025 synthetic-content labeling rules and how Bilibili implemented them; whether NRTA Order 16 on micro-dramas (effective 2026-09-01) reaches a 26-minute single film; Seedance API safety controls; OpenAI image policy and C2PA; copyright of training data and of the film’s reuse of *Soushen Ji* and *Hou Hanshu*; and 2025–2026 enforcement against similar AI political or historical shorts. Nothing below is a compliance determination about this specific work. As of 2026-09-07, no official source located for this research names the film as banned, taken down, or administratively penalized. [联合早报](https://www.zaobao.com.sg/entertainment/story20260903-9619222) (accessed 2026-09-07, confidence: High) reported that as of 2 September 2026 the video was still viewable and still listed in Bilibili’s weekly must-watch ranking. This environment could not open the Bilibili watch page (HTTP 412), so later status was not independently re-checked.

China’s labeling regime is the clearest hard overlay that already applied when the film went up. The Cyberspace Administration of China, MIIT, the Ministry of Public Security, and NRTA issued the *Measures for the Identification of Artificial Intelligence Generated and Synthesized Content* (国信办通字〔2025〕2号) on 7 March 2025; Article 14 states that the Measures take effect on 1 September 2025. [中国政府网](https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202503/content_7014286.htm) (accessed 2026-09-07, confidence: High) — **Primary**. Article 3 defines AI-generated or synthesized content as text, images, audio, video, and virtual scenes produced with AI, and splits identification into explicit marks that a viewer can perceive and implicit marks embedded in file data. Article 4, which points back to Article 17(1) of the 2022 *Provisions on the Administration of Deep Synthesis of Internet Information Services*, requires video providers to place a prominent prompt on the opening frames and around the player, and permits additional marks at the end or in the middle; downloads and exports must still carry a compliant explicit mark. Article 5 requires implicit marks in file metadata covering generation-attribute information, the provider’s name or code, and a content serial number, and encourages (but does not mandate) digital watermarks. Article 6 assigns distribution platforms four duties: if metadata says the file is synthetic, add a prominent public reminder; if metadata is silent but the user declared synthesis, add a “may be” reminder; if neither exists but the platform detects an explicit mark or other synthetic traces, treat the file as suspected synthesis and warn accordingly; and offer a declaration tool so users can self-identify. Article 10 requires users who publish synthetic content on a distribution service to declare it and use the platform’s identification function, and forbids malicious deletion, alteration, forgery, or concealment of required marks. The CAC Q&A of 14 March 2025 states that the Measures refine earlier algorithm, deep-synthesis, and generative-AI rules, that they travel with the mandatory national standard GB 45438-2025 *Cybersecurity technology — Identification methods for artificial intelligence generated and synthesized content*, and that both instruments took effect together on 1 September 2025 after about six months of transition. The Q&A also records that implicit marks in text and digital watermarks in multimedia were treated as technically hard or costly and were therefore not made universally mandatory in the Measures themselves, while metadata implicit marks were. [国家网信办答记者问](https://www.cac.gov.cn/2025-03/14/c_1743654685896173.htm) (accessed 2026-09-07, confidence: High) — **Primary**. GB 45438-2025 was approved in SAMR/SAC announcement No. 3 of 2025, published 28 February 2025, and implemented 1 September 2025. [标准网转述国标目录](https://www.biaozhun.org/guojia/365078.html) (accessed 2026-09-07, confidence: Medium, because the HTML directory is secondary to the paywalled GB text). The older deep-synthesis rule remains the statutory hook: Articles 16–18 of the *Provisions on the Administration of Deep Synthesis of Internet Information Services* already required non-intrusive technical marks, prominent marks where the service can confuse the public (including face generation and immersive scenes), and a ban on stripping those marks. [中国政府网国务院公报](https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2023/content_5741257.htm) (accessed 2026-09-07, confidence: High) — **Primary**.

Bilibili’s public implementation matches the user-declaration limb of Article 6 and Article 10 rather than a unique house statute. A Wuhan municipal CAC reprint of platform announcements around the 1 September 2025 effective date quotes a Bilibili notice of 29 August 2025: uploaders add the mark from the publish screen by opening 【创作声明】 and selecting 【该视频使用人工智能合成技术】; the site will add marks under community rules where users do not self-declare; users may not maliciously alter or hide the mark. [武汉市委网信办转中国经济网/澎湃](http://www.whwx.gov.cn/wlaq/wadt/202509/t20250902_2642783.shtml) (accessed 2026-09-07, confidence: Medium) — the wording is attributed to Bilibili but the page is a local-CAC reprint, not Bilibili.com. An on-platform governance post of 15 May 2026, issued during the CAC “Qinglang · rectify chaotic AI applications” campaign, says Bilibili had promoted the AI-identification control to a first-level page, would remind uploaders, and would apply a ladder of supplementary labeling, bounce-back for re-labeling, and takedown for omitted marks, plus stricter handling of AI fake voice-overs, face-swap deception, and AI fake news. [哔哩哔哩治理小分队](https://www.bilibili.com/opus/1202507670848798745) (accessed 2026-09-07, confidence: High) — **Primary as platform notice**. An older community post (the “主动添加内容标识” announcement) already listed “该视频使用人工智能合成技术” as the AIGC author declaration and ranked it first when only one declaration can be applied. [哔哩哔哩社区公告](https://www.bilibili.com/opus/840812291428450327) (accessed 2026-09-07, confidence: Medium). Lianhe Zaobao’s 3 September 2026 entertainment report is the established-media source that the finished film displayed a “含AI生成内容” chip and had no human performers. [联合早报](https://www.zaobao.com.sg/entertainment/story20260903-9619222) (accessed 2026-09-07, confidence: High for the chip as reported; Medium as to whether that chip is Bilibili’s user-declaration UI, a platform-detected “contains AI” overlay, or both). Public sources located for this axis do not show the implicit-metadata chain (Jimeng/Seedance → NLE → Bilibili transcode) surviving into the published MP4, so platform-side user declaration plus a visible chip is what can be documented; file-header GB 45438 fields after Bilibili re-encode were not inspected.

NRTA Order 16 is a different instrument, and its fit to this film is the main classification question rather than a settled “yes.” Director Cao Shumin signed *Measures for the Development and Administration of Micro-Dramas* as NRTA Order No. 16 on 30 July 2026; the text was published on the NRTA site on 31 July 2026 and Article 54 sets the effective date at 1 September 2026. [国家广播电视总局](https://www.nrta.gov.cn/art/2026/7/31/art_113_73785.html) (accessed 2026-09-07, confidence: High) — **Primary**. Article 2 defines a micro-drama as “a series with a clear theme and main line, a continuous and complete plot, and prominent characters, **each episode of which is shorter than twenty minutes**,” and then applies that definition to public distribution in China over websites, apps, broadcast channels, IPTV, and similar terminals. A single 26-minute film is longer than the per-episode cap and is not, on the ordinary meaning of 剧集, a series. That textual mismatch is the strongest public reason to treat Order 16’s licensing, filing, and per-episode numbering machinery as not automatically attaching to this upload. Two nearby clauses still matter as platform-side overlay rather than as a redefinition of the work. Article 33(3) tells micro-drama distributors to run content-management, inspection, and emergency-response measures for “non-professionally produced, relatively short-duration online audiovisual content **that has micro-drama characteristics**.” Article 34 requires makers and distributors of micro-dramas generated or produced with AI to follow national rules and to place a prompt mark in a prominent position **on each episode**. If a regulator later characterized the film as a one-episode micro-drama, or if Bilibili treated it as “micro-drama-like UGC,” those clauses would be the path; the Order itself does not say that a 26-minute one-shot film is a micro-drama. Timing also cuts against treating Order 16 as the rule that governed first publication: mainstream reporting places the Bilibili upload on 17 August 2026, fifteen days before the Order took effect, and the public text located for this research has no express retroactivity clause. [联合早报](https://www.zaobao.com.sg/entertainment/story20260903-9619222) (accessed 2026-09-07, confidence: High on the upload date). If the work *were* a micro-drama, Article 5 would be the next mapping: Category I covers large investment **or** special subjects whose main plot involves politics, military affairs, diplomacy, national security, united front, ethnicity, religion, justice, or public security; Categories II and III are ordinary subjects at medium and low investment. Article 25 then lists prohibited content, including (among other items) endangering national security and dignity, distorting ethnic history or historical figures, promoting historical nihilism, and spreading false information. Article 51 sends major revolutionary and major historical subjects to “relevant state provisions.” Those subject rules are real, but they are rules for works that first qualify as micro-dramas. Investment thresholds that the press attached to AI micro-dramas are not in the Order’s articles; they appear in NRTA Network Audiovisual Department “management tips.” Xinhua, attributing a 25 June 2026 tip, reported that AI micro-dramas at RMB 800,000 or above, or special subjects, would be managed as “key micro-dramas”; RMB 300,000–800,000 ordinary-subject AI works as “ordinary”; and under RMB 300,000 as “other,” with that tip effective 1 July 2026. [新华网客户端](https://app.xinhuanet.com/news/article.html?articleId=20260625e225478c1d4048dbaa0d75e3f28a7fb5) (accessed 2026-09-07, confidence: Medium). A later NetEase entertainment item claiming an 17 August 2026 tip restated the same RMB 800,000 / 300,000 AI bands but mapped them onto Order 16’s Category I/II/III vocabulary and added live-action bands of RMB 3 million and RMB 1 million; that item is explicitly “独家获悉” and is not the NRTA HTML text. [网易 / 影视一线](https://c.m.163.com/news/a/L4K741MQ0514R9P4.html) (accessed 2026-09-07, confidence: Low as to the August wording). Even if those bands applied, reporting on this film’s production cost is in the low thousands to about RMB 10,000 range and is itself Low-confidence; cost would not pull a work into Category I, whereas a finding that the *main plot involves politics* would. That finding is a content characterization, not a fact this research can make. Independently of Order 16, the film remains a piece of public internet audiovisual content on a licensed platform, and the *Interim Measures for the Management of Generative Artificial Intelligence Services* (CAC and six other agencies, effective 15 August 2023) still apply to provision and use of generative-AI services toward the Chinese public. Article 4(1) requires adherence to the core socialist values and forbids generating content that incites subversion, endangers national security, harms the national image, or constitutes other information prohibited by law; Article 2, second paragraph, says that where the state has other rules for using generative AI in news publishing, film and television production, or literary and artistic creation, those other rules prevail. [中国政府网](https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202307/content_6891752.htm) (accessed 2026-09-07, confidence: High) — **Primary**.

Seedance’s published safety surface is a product-control layer, not a Chinese content statute, and it is documented more completely on BytePlus (international ModelArk / LAS) than on the China Volcengine HTML pages this environment could fully render. BytePlus LAS documentation for Seedance 2.x states that the series “do not support directly uploading reference images or videos containing real human faces”; authorized likenesses go through an allowlisted asset library as `asset://<ASSET_ID>`. The same page says the generated-video pre-signed link is valid for 24 hours, that the task `id` is retained for seven days from `created_at`, and that `watermark` is an optional boolean defaulting to `false`. [BytePlus LAS — Enhanced/basic video generation](https://docs.byteplus.com/en/docs/byteplus_las/video_gen_enhanced) (accessed 2026-09-07, confidence: High) — **Primary**. BytePlus’s 6 August 2026 product blog for Dreamina Seedance 2.5 repeats the real-face restriction, describes a ModelArk library of more than 10,000 virtual humans, and states that vetted clients may complete real-person verification and likeness authorization in the console; it also says BytePlus has added C2PA Content Credentials on top of existing visible watermarks, plus filters against unauthorized intellectual property, and that clients must follow the GenAI Acceptable Use Policy. [BytePlus blog](https://www.byteplus.com/en/blog/dreamina-seedance2-5) (accessed 2026-09-07, confidence: High) — **Primary for the international product**. China-side Ark documentation titles confirm a “virtual portrait library” for Seedance 2.0 and 2.5, a path to register authorized real-person assets, an implicit-watermark feature for API customers, and “copyright and portrait material usage rules.” Search snippets of the virtual-portrait page (last updated 10 August 2026) say Seedance 2.0/2.5 do not support direct upload of real-face references; that videos generated by those models are treated as trusted assets that can be fed back for edit/extend without the face block; and that a public virtual-portrait library is offered instead. [火山方舟文档目录](https://docs.volcengine.com/docs/82379/2223965?lang=zh) (accessed 2026-09-07, confidence: Medium; the body did not render in this fetch). Search snippets of the implicit-watermark guide say Ark can write GB-style metadata fields such as Label, ContentProducer, and ContentPropagator when a custom inference endpoint is configured with the provider’s name and unified social credit code, and that marked outputs can be checked on a national identification-detection platform; video is listed as supported. [火山方舟隐式水印文档](https://docs.volcengine.com/docs/82379/1810470?lang=zh) (accessed 2026-09-07, confidence: Medium). Those controls are relevant to *this* film mainly in the negative: reporting describes GPT Image 2 fictional faces driving Seedance 2.5, not a celebrity likeness upload, so the real-face block would not be expected to fire; a creator who turned `watermark=false` (the documented default) and then cut 50-plus clips in a local NLE would be expected to drop both visible vendor marks and some implicit headers. URL expiry and seven-day task retention are operational facts for anyone reproducing the pipeline: the API is not an archive. Whether the uploader used Jimeng/Doubao, Volcengine Ark (`doubao-seedance-2-5-260628`), or BytePlus (`dreamina-seedance-2-5-260628`) is not established in public sources; BytePlus is stated as unavailable in the United States, which does not decide the China-side path. [BytePlus blog](https://www.byteplus.com/en/blog/dreamina-seedance2-5) (accessed 2026-09-07, confidence: High for the US-availability note only).

OpenAI’s published image rules and provenance stack sit on the still-frame side of the same pipeline. The current Usage Policies forbid using someone’s likeness, including a photorealistic image or voice, without consent “in ways that could confuse authenticity,” and separately forbid political campaigning, lobbying, and foreign or domestic election interference or demobilization. [OpenAI Usage Policies](https://openai.com/policies/usage-policies/) (accessed 2026-09-07, confidence: High from search-indexed official text; the live page timed out in this environment). Service terms for visual capabilities add that users may not use image or video inputs to identify a person or to reproduce a likeness without express consent and necessary rights. [OpenAI Service terms](https://openai.com/policies/service-terms/) (accessed 2026-09-07, confidence: Medium). That is a different posture from DALL·E-era blanket blocks on public figures. The March 2025 addendum to the GPT-4o system card on native image generation states that the model can often depict an adult public figure from text alone; that OpenAI was “not blocking” that capability at launch; that public figures may opt out; and that the change was intended to allow “helpful and beneficial uses in areas like educational, historical, satirical and political speech,” while still seeking to block photorealistic images of public-figure minors and policy-violating violent, hateful, illicit, or erotic material. [OpenAI, Addendum to GPT-4o System Card: Native image generation](https://cdn.openai.com/11998be9-5319-4302-bfbf-1167e093f1fb/Native_Image_Generation_System_Card.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-07, confidence: High) — **Primary**. GPT Image 2 (`gpt-image-2`) is the 2026 successor in the same native-image line; API access requires organization verification. [OpenAI API — Image generation](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/image-generation) (accessed 2026-09-07, confidence: High). A historical-costume fiction that does not impersonate a living person is closer to the uses the 2025 addendum lists than to a campaign deepfake; whether any particular frame of Zhang Jue or a eunuch “confused authenticity” is not a question public policy pages answer. Provenance is documented more tightly than the likeness rule. OpenAI’s Help Center states that supported images from ChatGPT, Codex, and the API carry both C2PA Content Credentials and SynthID watermarks; that these signals indicate origin, not accuracy, ownership, or correct context; that C2PA metadata can be stripped by platforms, editors, and format conversion; and that SynthID is embedded in the pixels and may survive some transformations. A public checker is at openai.com/verify. [OpenAI Help Center](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/8912793-provenance-signals-content-credentials-synthid-in-openai-generated-content) (accessed 2026-09-07, confidence: High) — **Primary**. The API guide for `POST /v1/content_provenance_checks` repeats that a `not_detected` result is not proof of human authorship, that the tool does not detect other vendors’ models, and that compression, cropping, screenshots, and re-encoding can erase or weaken signals. [OpenAI API — Content provenance](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/content-provenance) (accessed 2026-09-07, confidence: High) — **Primary**. For this film, any GPT Image 2 still that was re-encoded into a Seedance reference, then into an NLE timeline, then into Bilibili’s transcode, should be assumed to have lost C2PA headers; SynthID in those stills, if it survived at all, would not automatically label the finished video as OpenAI-origin. OpenAI’s political-campaigning help page is about scaled campaign messaging and election tooling, not about a fictional Eastern-Han allegory, and is cited here only to bound the Usage Policy term. [OpenAI Help Center — Political Campaigning Restrictions](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001255-political-campaigning-restrictions) (accessed 2026-09-07, confidence: Medium).

Copyright splits into three different questions that public sources should not collapse. First, the *Soushen Ji* and *Hou Hanshu* passages the film tags (temple-wall yellow man, “wood failing to be straight,” Liang Boxia, grass in human form, Yellow Turban slogans) are from works whose authors died in the fourth and fifth centuries. Under Article 23 of the *Copyright Law of the People’s Republic of China* (2020 revision), a natural person’s economic rights last for the author’s life plus fifty years, ending on 31 December of the fiftieth year; those terms on Gan Bao and Fan Ye expired many centuries ago. Article 22, however, states that attribution, revision, and integrity rights are not time-limited. [国家版权局](https://www.ncac.gov.cn/xxfb/flfg/flfg_532/202103/t20210309_50530.html) (accessed 2026-09-07, confidence: High) — **Primary**. In ordinary publishing practice, unannotated classical text is treated as public-domain source material; a modern annotated edition, translation, or typesetting can still carry neighboring rights (layout design is protected for ten years under Article 37). This research did not find a rights-holder claim against the film’s classical quotations. Integrity-right theory could be raised against a transformative retelling, but located sources do not show such a claim, and Chinese public discussion of this work has treated the classical tags as literary intertext, not as an infringement file. Second, training-data copyright is a provider-side duty under Article 7 of the 2023 Interim Measures: providers must use data and base models of lawful origin and must not infringe others’ intellectual-property rights when IP is involved. [中国政府网](https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202307/content_6891752.htm) (accessed 2026-09-07, confidence: High). The Copyright Law’s Article 24 fair-use list does not name model training or text-and-data mining. Commentary collected for this axis notes a split in 2024–2026 civil judgments (Hangzhou Ultraman-style training/fair-use reasoning versus Shanghai Medusa LoRA-style assessments) and treats that split as unresolved. [中伦](https://www.zhonglun.com/research/articles/56673.html) (accessed 2026-09-07, confidence: Medium, law-firm commentary not a judgment). The uploader of this film is a user of Seedance and GPT Image 2, not the trainer of those models; any training-corpus risk sits with ByteDance and OpenAI under their own terms and under Article 7, not with a documented lawsuit about this title. A separate, untested resemblance risk is that GPT Image 2 / Seedance outputs in a Han-costume register may echo still-protected contemporary costume dramas; no source located for this research alleges a specific rightsholder clash. Third, whether the finished 26-minute audiovisual work is itself a “work” under Article 3 depends on human original contribution. Public reporting describes a human script adapted from the uploader’s earlier essay, human editorial assembly of generated shots, and disclosed tool names; that fact pattern is the kind of human-contribution record Chinese commentary discusses, but there is no published court finding on this title’s copyrightability.

Enforcement in 2025–2026 against *similar* AI historical or political shorts is documented at the campaign level, not as a docket against this film. In April 2025 CAC launched a three-month “Qinglang · rectify abuse of AI technology” special action. Stage two listed, among other targets, using AI to mock, smear, distort, or alienate public figures **or historical figures**, and using AI to fabricate rumors on current politics and public policy. [国家网信办](https://www.cac.gov.cn/2025-04/30/c_1747719097461951.htm) (accessed 2026-09-07, confidence: High) — **Primary**. Stage-one results published on 20 June 2025 claimed disposal of more than 3,500 noncompliant AI products, 960,000 pieces of illegal information, and 3,700 accounts, with an emphasis on face-swap tools and missing content labels. [国家网信办](https://www.cac.gov.cn/2025-06/20/c_1752129980667315.htm) (accessed 2026-09-07, confidence: High). In April 2026 CAC launched a four-month “Qinglang · rectify chaotic AI applications” action. Stage one targeted, among other items, models that had not completed filing, unsafe training corpora including unauthorized text/image/AV data, and failure to implement the 2025 Identification Measures and GB 45438 (including tutorials and tools for stripping marks). Stage two, described in the same 30 April 2026 notice, led with “using AI to ‘remix’ the classics and generate ‘digital slop’”: distorting or deconstructing excellent traditional culture, historical figures, or historical allusions; abusing AI to remix classical novels or classic film and television; inserting vulgar or violent content; mocking the spirit of the classics; and overturning important characters’ established images. [国家网信办](https://www.cac.gov.cn/2026-04/30/c_1779289298718765.htm) (accessed 2026-09-07, confidence: High) — **Primary**. Stage-one results of 6 July 2026 claimed disposal of more than 14,000 noncompliant AI products, 6 million pieces of illegal information, and 26,000 accounts. [国家网信办](https://www.cac.gov.cn/2026-07/06/c_1785081384384987.htm) (accessed 2026-09-07, confidence: High). Stage-two results of 2 September 2026 — five days before this research date — claimed a cumulative 5.61 million pieces of illegal information cleaned, 49,000 accounts handled, and 2,400 websites or apps disposed of, and named Bilibili among platforms that had upgraded multimodal detection and published governance notices. The first typical-case cluster is AI remixes of *Romance of the Three Kingdoms* and *Journey to the West* that overturned characterizations and invented low-quality sensational plots for traffic; the named accounts are Weibo, Douyin, Kuaishou, and WeChat handles, not a Bilibili historical allegory. Other clusters are fake disaster footage, celebrity face-swap erotica, horror/vulgar bulk posts (one Bilibili account is listed in that violence/vulgar bucket, not in the classics-remix bucket), harm to minors, and AI-operated astroturf. [国家网信办](https://www.cac.gov.cn/2026-09/02/c_1790099041364574.htm) (accessed 2026-09-07, confidence: High) — **Primary**. Those campaigns show that “AI + classical China + inverted historical figures” is an active enforcement *category*. They do not show that this film was placed in that category. The 2 September typical cases emphasize low-quality traffic remixes and vulgar inversion; reporting on 《把人写成了妖》 emphasizes a labeled, 26-minute narrative with an on-screen fiction disclaimer and a creator comment that the plot is artistic processing, not real history. Equating the two would be an inference this research does not make. Adjacent, still-in-force rules that a platform moderator could theoretically cite, without any finding that they were cited, include Order 16 Article 25’s historical-nihilism and false-information clauses (if the work were treated as a micro-drama), Interim Measures Article 4(1), and the Qinglang 2026 “remix the classics / distort historical figures” bullet. Public sources located as of 2026-09-07 do not record an NRTA, CAC, or Bilibili enforcement action that names this title.

> Conflicts noted:
> Order 16 Article 2 (episode < 20 minutes, must be a 剧集) versus press and platform habit of calling any short AI narrative a “微短剧,” and versus Article 33(3)’s looser “micro-drama characteristics” platform duty. June 2026 NRTA tip language (“重点 / 普通 / 其他微短剧,” Xinhua) versus August 2026 entertainment reporting that maps the same AI investment bands onto Order 16 Categories I–III (NetEase “独家获悉”). BytePlus documents C2PA on Seedance 2.5 internationally; Volcengine China pages describe GB 45438-style metadata implicit marks, and the two provenance systems are not shown to be the same bytes. `watermark` defaults to false in the LAS API while BytePlus marketing describes “existing visible watermarks.” OpenAI Usage Policies still bar likeness use that confuses authenticity and bar campaign use; the 2025 GPT-4o image addendum allows adult public figures and lists historical, satirical, and political speech as intended beneficial uses. Economic copyright in *Soushen Ji* / *Hou Hanshu* has expired; Article 22 moral rights are formally perpetual. Lianhe Zaobao’s “含AI生成内容” chip versus Bilibili’s exact declaration string “该视频使用人工智能合成技术” — likely the same UI family, not independently pixel-matched. No source located for this research claims the film was banned; that absence is not a positive official clearance.
