## Funding and Capital Activity

### Transmission Channels

Five channels can, in theory, carry a BRICS/SCO dollar challenge into US equity prices. Only two of them map a *measured* official-reserve object onto US discount rates, and their documented yield effects are tens of basis points, not the 100–150 bp “pure discounting” shock that duration arithmetic needs for a 20–30 percent PE compression. [Gormsen and Lazarus, NBER w34814](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w34814/w34814.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Established**.

Channel 1 is official Treasury selling raising yields and compressing PE. Beltran et al. estimate that a $100 billion monthly drop in foreign official inflows raises the 5-year yield about 40–60 bp in the short run and about 20 bp in the long run. [Federal Reserve IFDP 1041](https://www.federalreserve.gov/pubs/ifdp/2012/1041/ifdp1041.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary**. Neely’s St. Louis Fed scenario for sale of China’s entire then-$3 trillion reserve portfolio is only 19–32 bp along the curve. [Neely, St. Louis Fed WP 2017-001](https://files.stlouisfed.org/files/htdocs/wp/2017/2017-001.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary**. The 2014–16 Chinese reserve-sale episode moved the 10-year yield *down* 11 bp, the wrong sign for a dump-crash story. [USCC staff paper](https://www.uscc.gov/sites/default/files/Research/U.S.%20Financial%20Exposure%20to%20China.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary**. This suggests a ≥20 percent index drawdown through this channel alone is not implied by published long-run elasticities applied to any historically observed official sale.

Channel 2 is foreign selling of US equities. Officials held $2.23 trillion of US equities on 30 June 2025 versus $19.86 trillion of all foreign equity holdings. [U.S. Treasury](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0482) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary**. Gabaix and Koijen’s inelastic-markets multiplier of about 5 would turn a complete official equity liquidation into a roughly 21 percent price move only if that domestic multiplier applies one-for-one to foreign official sales — which is not documented. [Gabaix and Koijen, NBER w28967](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w28967/w28967.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Established**. Froot, O’Connell, and Seasholes find foreign inflows do **not** forecast developed-country returns. [NBER w6687](https://www.nber.org/papers/w6687) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Established**.

Channel 3 is a weaker dollar. S&P 500 foreign sales are about 40–44 percent of sales, so dollar depreciation is an *earnings tailwind*, not a crash channel. [S&P DJI](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/documents/research/sp-500-global-sales-2018.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary**. Gopinath estimates a 10 percent dollar depreciation raises US CPI only 0.4–0.7 percentage points over two years. [NBER Digest / Gopinath w21646](https://www.nber.org/digest/jan16/cross-country-differences-exchange-rate-effects-inflation) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Established**. The 2022 ≥20 percent equity drawdown came with a *stronger* dollar.

Channel 4 is loss of “exorbitant privilege.” Krishnamurthy and Vissing-Jørgensen estimate Treasuries yielded on the order of 70 bp less than they would without convenience. [NBER w12881](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w12881/w12881.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Established**. Treating the entire convenience yield as a one-step equity discount-rate shock is not in the 2000–2026 data.

Channel 5 is risk-off *into* dollar assets. This channel can produce a ≥30 percent equity crash — 2008 is the existence proof — but the cause was a financial-system crash, not BRICS reserve diversification, and the dollar *rose*. [McCauley and McGuire, BIS](https://www.bis.org/publications/dollar-appreciation-2008-safe-haven-carry-trades-dollar-shortage-and-overhedging_1.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary**.

> Conflicts noted: Official-flow yield elasticities disagree by an order of magnitude by horizon. TIC equity “sales” can have the opposite sign of SLT transactions. April 2025 (dollar, bonds, and equities down together) versus 2008 (dollar up, equities down).
> Gaps: No Fed/IMF/BIS paper estimates a BRICS-specific dump as an identified post-2016 shock. No published TIC-equity-flow elasticity for the S&P in “percent per $100 billion.”

### Who Owns US Equities

The June 2025 TIC survey measured foreign holdings of US equities at $19.860 trillion, about 32 percent of the listed market. Official institutions held $2.230 trillion of those equities. [U.S. Treasury SB0482](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0482) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary**. Mainland China held $344 billion of US equities (0.55 percent of the mid-2025 market). Core BRICS summed to about $429 billion (0.68 percent). [TIC SHL Table 1](https://ticdata.treasury.gov/Publish/shlptab1.html) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary**. China mainland equity is 2.7 percent of a 20 percent cap-loss hole; core BRICS is 3.4 percent. Official foreign equity worldwide is 18 percent of that hole.

The largest recorded foreign equity holders are Cayman, Canada, the UK, Ireland, and Luxembourg — custodial and allied private centers, not BRICS official accounts. Foreigners were still *adding* to US equities: $805.1 billion of net purchases in the twelve months through June 2026, of which official net purchases were $114.3 billion. [U.S. Treasury SB0606](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0606) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary**. Households and nonprofits held $64.8 trillion of corporate equities directly plus indirectly at Q1 2026. [FRED](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/BOGZ1LM153064475Q) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary**. Roughly 68–71 percent of the US equity stock is held domestically.

A de-dollarization story that runs through China’s official sector is, on these prints, a **Treasury and agency** story, not an S&P ownership story. China’s US debt book is much larger than its equity book. [Treasury SB0482](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0482) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary**.

> Conflicts noted: TIC versus Z.1 equity totals differ because TIC includes fund shares. Country TIC is custodial residence, not beneficial owner. CAPE prints differ by as-of date.
> Gaps: No official-versus-private equity split by country. Ultimate BRICS beneficial ownership booked through Cayman or London is not in the tables.

## Historical Analogues

The historical record does not show that losing reserve-currency status, a dollar shock, or foreign official selling, by itself, produced a US-style equity crash.

The Nixon shock of 15 August 1971 produced a “Nixon rally”: the Dow rose 3.85 percent the next session; calendar 1971 S&P total return was +14.22 percent. [New York Fed](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/monthly_review/1971_pdf/09_2_71.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary**. [Damodaran](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/datafile/histretSP.html) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Established**. The 1973–74 bear (−48.2 percent peak to trough) overlapped Bretton Woods’ collapse, but Fed History lists oil, Vietnam financing, food prices, and the boom — not a foreign official dump — as the inflation complex. [Yardeni](https://old.yardeni.com/wp-content/uploads/BullBearTables.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Established**. [Federal Reserve History](https://www.federalreservehistory.org/essays/oil-shock-of-1973-74) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary**.

Plaza 1985: the dollar fell about 40 percent over 1985–87 while S&P total returns were +31.24 percent (1985) and +18.49 percent (1986). [Frankel](https://www.bakerinstitute.org/sites/default/files/2015-09/import/WorkingPaper-Plaza-Frankel-092815.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Established**. [Damodaran](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/datafile/histretSP.html) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Established**. 2008 and 2022 were the two worst recent US bears and both came with a *stronger* dollar. [US Treasury FX report, December 2008](https://home.treasury.gov/system/files/206/FXREPORT--FinalDecember2008.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary**. [Reuters, 2022](https://www.reuters.com/markets/us/futures-slip-last-trading-day-torrid-year-2022-12-30/) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Established**. China’s 2015–16 reserve sales (on the order of $800 billion of reserves, about $210 billion of Treasuries) produced a correction, not a crash; 2015–16 calendar S&P total returns were +1.38 and +11.77 percent. [USCC](https://www.uscc.gov/sites/default/files/Research/U.S.%20Financial%20Exposure%20to%20China.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary**. [Damodaran](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/datafile/histretSP.html) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Established**.

April 2025 tariffs *are* documented as dollar-down plus stocks-down, and they are a US policy shock, not a BRICS reserve switch. The S&P dropped about 11–13 percent and recovered in weeks. [San Francisco Fed](https://www.frbsf.org/research-and-insights/publications/economic-letter/2025/10/market-reactions-to-tariff-announcements/) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary**. [BIS WP 1282](https://www.bis.org/publ/work1282.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary**. Sterling’s multi-decade reserve loss was largely complete before the UK’s 1972–74 equity wipeout, which sources attribute to oil, inflation, property, and secondary banking. [UK Commons Library](https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/pound-in-your-pocket-devaluation-50-years-on/) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary**. [Bank of England](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/quarterly-bulletin/1978/q2/the-secondary-banking-crisis-and-the-bank-of-englands-support-operations) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary**.

The same window in which the dollar’s reserve share fell from about 71 percent (1999) to about 58 percent (2024) saw large US equity gains. Named 2010–2015 de-dollarization crash calls failed on timing. [IMF WP 22/58](https://www.elibrary.imf.org/view/journals/001/2022/058/article-A001-en.xml) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary**. [Damodaran](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/datafile/histretSP.html) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Established**.

> Conflicts noted: Peak-to-trough versus calendar returns. BIS and SF Fed 2025 drop estimates differ (13 vs 11 percent).
> Gaps: No primary event study of UK equities around the 1949 or 1967 devaluations. No documented BRICS reserve-substitution episode in 2022–2026 that coincides with a US equity crash.

## Momentum and Adjacent Forces

### USD–Equity Correlation and Offsets

The long-run USD–S&P relationship is not a stable negative number that would make “dollar down ⇒ stocks crash” an empirical regularity. The sign flips with the shock. Global risk-off and Fed tightening typically send the dollar up and equities down. Orderly dollar depreciation under stable US financial conditions typically accompanies rising S&P prices. The two fall together when the shock is a US policy or US-risk event — April 2025 is the documented modern case. [San Francisco Fed](https://www.frbsf.org/research-and-insights/publications/economic-letter/2025/10/market-reactions-to-tariff-announcements/) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary**.

Plaza 1985–87 is the existence proof that a large, policy-sponsored dollar decline need not crash the S&P: the dollar fell about 40 percent while S&P total returns compounded about +65 percent over 1985–87. [Frankel](https://www.bakerinstitute.org/sites/default/files/2015-09/import/WorkingPaper-Plaza-Frankel-092815.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Established**. [Damodaran](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/datafile/histretSP.html) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Established**. The 2002–07 dollar descent of about 26 percent in real trade-weighted terms overlapped an S&P compounding of about +82 percent from 2003–07. [CRS RL34582](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/20110415_RL34582_bdf52352969d87f944d4996278ff281c78ebdfd3.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary**. The two large modern US bears, 2008 and 2022, arrived with a *stronger* dollar. [BIS Bulletin 62](https://www.bis.org/publ/bisbull62.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary**. ECB staff identify a global risk shock that *appreciates* the dollar and *lowers* equity prices. [ECB WP 2628](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/pub/pdf/scpwps/ecb.wp2628~1f46b7ba26.en.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary**.

Four offsets keep an observed-size official sale from mapping into a ≥20 percent S&P crash. First, Fed staff estimate that 1 percent dollar *appreciation* reduces S&P profit growth by about ¾ of a percent, so a weaker dollar is an earnings tailwind; the Mag7 supplied 55 percent of the three-year S&P total return through 2025. [Federal Reserve FEDS 2024-049](https://www.federalreserve.gov/econres/feds/files/2024049pap.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary**. [S&P DJI, Dec 2025](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/commentary/article/us-equities-market-attributes/) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary**. Second, households and nonprofits held $64.8 trillion of corporate equities at Q1 2026; residual household equity *transactions* were about $864 billion in calendar 2025. [FRED](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/BOGZ1LM153064475Q) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary**. Third, the FIMA Repo Facility and dollar swap lines can convert a *liquidity-driven* official Treasury sale into a Fed repo; they do not absorb a *permanent* reserve-composition switch. [Federal Reserve, FIMA](https://www.federalreserve.gov/monetarypolicy/fima-repo-facility.htm) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary**. Fourth, dollar stablecoins held $153 billion of T-bills by December 2025 — a front-end bid, two orders of magnitude smaller than the $3.5 trillion official long-term Treasury stock, with limited spillover to the 10-year. [BIS WP 1270](https://www.bis.org/publications/working-paper-1270-stablecoins-and-safe-asset-prices.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary**.

This suggests that for an observed-size official sale (low-hundreds of billions of Treasuries, or the $429 billion core-BRICS equity book), published stocks and coefficients do not imply a ≥20 percent S&P crash. For a permanent sale of the full official long-term Treasury book, FIMA is the wrong instrument and Mag7 earnings offset translation rather than duration.

> Conflicts noted: 2022 S&P prints range from −18.04 percent (Damodaran total) to −21.8 percent (Silverblatt June 13). 2025 two-day drop: SF Fed 11 percent versus other houses’ 13 percent. Household equity transactions are a Z.1 residual.
> Gaps: No official “percent S&P per 10 percent dollar” *price* elasticity. No published test of whether FIMA plus swaps plus stablecoins can absorb a multi-trillion *permanent* official diversification.

Damodaran’s implied ERP on 1 September 2026 is 4.14 percent against a then-4.75 percent Treasury, so the implied cost of equity is 8.89 percent. [FactSet](https://advantage.factset.com/hubfs/Website/Resources%20Section/Research%20Desk/Earnings%20Insight/EarningsInsight_090426.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Established**. That is the bar official selling has to clear. Neely’s $3 trillion China-FX sale is 19–32 bp. Beltran’s share-of-outstanding specification on a $31.8 trillion marketable Treasury stock puts a full *global* official liquidation at about 59–71 bp. J.P. Morgan’s “not our base case” elasticity (>33 bp per $300 billion) puts the $0.99 trillion China–India–Brazil custodial Treasury book near 109 bp — close to the *linear* 20 percent bar, short of the Gordon-exact 104 bp 20 percent bar, and short of every 30 percent figure. [Neely, St. Louis Fed WP 2017-001](https://files.stlouisfed.org/files/htdocs/wp/2017/2017-001.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary**. [Federal Reserve IFDP 1041](https://www.federalreserve.gov/pubs/ifdp/2012/1041/ifdp1041.htm) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary**. [J.P. Morgan](https://www.jpmorgan.com/insights/global-research/currencies/de-dollarization) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary**. Krishnamurthy et al.’s complete-disappearance counterfactual is about +90 bp on long-term US rates — on the 20 percent linear bar, below the Gordon-exact 20 percent bar, and not a BRICS sale. [Stanford GSB](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/insights/dollars-dominance-eroding) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Established**.

The $0.43 trillion core-BRICS *equity* stock is 0.62 percent of the $68.9 trillion listed market. Even Gabaix–Koijen’s multiplier of 5 would turn a complete core-BRICS equity liquidation into a roughly 3 percent price move. [SIFMA](https://www.sifma.org/news/blog/2026-capital-markets-fact-book-key-findings) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Established**. [Gabaix and Koijen, NBER w28967](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w28967/w28967.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Established**.

A recession crash is a different equation. If forward earnings fall 15–25 percent and the multiple is unchanged, the index is already most of the way to a 20 percent bear without any yield shock. No Beltran, Neely, or JPM estimate *identifies* official Treasury selling as a US earnings recession. The 2008 ≥30 percent crash ran through a financial-system failure and a *falling* safe yield — an ERP event, not a term-premium dump.

Forced delisting would reprice those names. It would not subtract $1.1 trillion from Apple or Microsoft. [USCC, March 2025](https://www.uscc.gov/sites/default/files/2025-03/Chinese_Companies_Listed_on_US_Stock_Exchanges_03_2025.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary**. ## Scenario Map

Horizon: 2026–2030. The object is not “will US stocks fall” but “would a BRICS/SCO de-dollarization *cause* a US equity crash.” No official house, IMF, Fed, or BIS note assigns a numeric probability that BRICS or the SCO produce a ≥20 percent or ≥30 percent US equity decline by 2030. Rankings below are **synthesis**.

### Bear Case (no BRICS-caused crash)

De-dollarization continues as a slow corridor project. Any US equity decline that does occur is produced by something else. Governor Waller said he does not expect the dollar to lose reserve status “anytime soon.” [Federal Reserve, Waller](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/speech/waller20240215a.htm) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary**. IMF COFER put the dollar at 57.13 percent of allocated FX reserves in 2026Q1, the euro at 20.03 percent, and the renminbi at 1.99 percent. [IMF Data Brief, July 2026](https://data.imf.org/en/news/imf%20data%20brief%20july%201) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary**. Prasad, Liao, and Zhang told Jackson Hole that diversification away from US financial assets “is not borne out in the data.” [Kansas City Fed, August 2026](https://www.kansascityfed.org/documents/18555/prasad.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary**. This is the case that matches the 2026 stock-take. **Signal.**

### Base Case

The consensus through 2030 is a still-first dollar, a slow reserve-share drift, and a US equity market that can cheapen or rotate without a BRICS-caused crash. Goldman: “The Dollar will not be replaced anytime soon, but it should still depreciate” via “a cheapening of US local assets or a weakening currency, most likely both” — “not contingent on an erosion in Dollar dominance.” [Goldman Sachs via CFS](https://www.cfsrating.com/media/ujigt4nb/global-markets-analyst_-dollar-dominance-and-dollar-depreciation-moving-on-different-tracks-trivedi_jenkins.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary**. **Forecast.** J.P. Morgan says *if* structural de-dollarization occurred, US equities would suffer; evidence “remains limited” and a Treasury-dump yield shock “is not our base case.” [J.P. Morgan](https://www.jpmorgan.com/insights/global-research/currencies/de-dollarization) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary**. BlackRock stayed overweight US stocks after April 2025. [BlackRock](https://www.blackrock.com/us/individual/literature/market-commentary/weekly-investment-commentary-en-us-20251013-us-dollar-drop-not-that-unusual-yet.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary**. Vanguard’s muted 10-year US equity return is a *valuation* forecast, not a reserve-status forecast. [Vanguard VEMO 2026](https://corporate.vanguard.com/content/dam/corp/research/pdf/isg_vemo_2026.pdf?stream=top) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary**.

### Bull Case (crash from this cause)

A crash *from this cause* is a stacked-shock path, not the continuation of observed de-dollarization. No named official or bulge-bracket house treats that stack as the expected 2026–2030 trajectory. **Speculative** on occurrence.

The arithmetic: a ≥20 percent index move from this cause needs a 100-plus bp *pure* discounting shock that published long-run official-sale elasticities do not deliver from any historically observed dump, or a multi-trillion-dollar net foreign *equity* sale that is not in the 2000–2026 TIC record. A ≥30 percent move needs a further increment — 150 bp of pure discounting, or that rate shock plus a cash-flow recession.

Five accelerants appear in the literature as watch items, not as a forecast: (1) a US fiscal tipping point beyond the CBO baseline (deficit 5.8 percent of GDP in 2026; debt 108 percent of GDP in 2030), which is a US self-harm shock; [CBO](https://www.cbo.gov/publication/62105) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary**. (2) a completed Fed-independence break — the 2026 Cook ruling blocked the first ouster attempt; [Fortune, 29 June 2026](https://fortune.com/2026/06/29/supreme-court-fed-independence-lisa-cook-us-debt-treasury-bonds/) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Established**. (3) a Gulf SWF dump — identifiable Saudi plus UAE Treasuries were about $257 billion at end-June 2026, under JPM’s $300 billion / 33 bp unit; [TIC Table 5](https://ticdata.treasury.gov/resource-center/data-chart-center/tic/Documents/slt_table5.txt) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary**. (4) an Indian strategic flip — Jaishankar said India has “no interest in weakening the dollar at all”; [Reuters, 7 December 2024](https://www.reuters.com/world/india/brics-have-no-interest-weakening-usd-indian-foreign-minister-says-2024-12-07/) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Established**. (5) Chinese capital-account convertibility, still described by SAFE as a “systemic undertaking” with “phased implementation.” [SAFE](http://www.safe.gov.cn/en/2026/0617/2439.html) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Primary**.

If US fiscal stress plus a Fed-independence break produce a ≥20 percent equity decline, BRICS summits will claim credit. Eichengreen’s line is that whether the dollar keeps its role “will depend not simply on the United States’ relations with Russia, China, or the BRICS countries” but on US debts and politics. [Eichengreen, Project Syndicate](https://www.project-syndicate.org/commentary/will-us-dollar-global-hegemony-last-by-barry-eichengreen-2023-09) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Established**. The causal label would still be US self-harm. **Synthesis.**

Fitch’s 9 September 2026 stress test is the live mis-attribution risk. Business Insider’s recap describes a 35 percent drop in *AI-sector* prices over six months, a 2027 US recession, and an explicit note that this “was not their base case for the AI trade.” Secondary recaps collapse that into a 35 percent *US stock* crash. [Business Insider / Fitch](https://www.businessinsider.com/ai-stock-crash-us-recession-global-stagnation-fitch-ratings-2026-9) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High) **Established**. That is an AI-capex re-evaluation, not a BRICS dump.

**Falsifiable markers** that would move this axis off the base case: a measured 100-plus bp *pure* discounting shock that Fed/BIS/IMF event studies attribute to official or BRICS selling; and/or TIC-documented net foreign equity *transactions* on the order of $2 trillion that are not a valuation residual. ## Key Findings

The first finding is a category error. According to the June 2025 TIC survey, core BRICS holders of US equities sum to about $429 billion, or 0.68 percent of the mid-2025 US listed market; mainland China is $344 billion. [U.S. Treasury SB0482](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0482) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High). Official institutions worldwide hold $2.23 trillion of US equities — 18 percent of a 20 percent cap-loss hole — and that book is not identified as BRICS in the public tables. Foreigners as a group hold about one-third of the market, but they were still *buying*: $805 billion of net US-equity purchases in the year to June 2026. [U.S. Treasury SB0606](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0606) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High). This suggests a BRICS official-plus-private *equity* dump cannot, on published stocks, produce a Mishkin-White or S&P 20 percent bear unless it is only the spark for a much larger Western and leveraged sale.

The second finding is that the live object is Treasuries, and even there the published elasticities do not clear a crash bar. At September 2026 valuations (forward P/E 19.5; implied ERP 4.14 percent; 10-year near 5 percent), a cash-flow-held 20 percent S&P drawdown is an 80–105 bp *pure* discount-rate shock. [FactSet](https://advantage.factset.com/hubfs/Website/Resources%20Section/Research%20Desk/Earnings%20Insight/EarningsInsight_090426.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High). [Damodaran](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/home.htm) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High). Neely’s full-China-reserve scenario is 19–32 bp. Beltran’s share-of-outstanding specification puts a full *global* official liquidation at about 60–70 bp. J.P. Morgan’s non-base-case coefficient can put a $0.99 trillion China–India–Brazil custodial book near 100 bp, which is the only published path that even *touches* a 20 percent linear duration move — and JPM says it is not the base case. [Neely](https://files.stlouisfed.org/files/htdocs/wp/2017/2017-001.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High). [J.P. Morgan](https://www.jpmorgan.com/insights/global-research/currencies/de-dollarization) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High). The 2015–16 Chinese sale moved the 10-year the *wrong* way. [USCC](https://www.uscc.gov/sites/default/files/Research/U.S.%20Financial%20Exposure%20to%20China.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High).

The third finding is historical. A weaker dollar has been compatible with rising US stocks (1971, 1985–86, 2003–07, calendar 2025) and with falling US stocks (1973–74, 1987, April 2025). A stronger dollar coincided with the two worst recent US bears (2008, 2022). [Damodaran](https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/New_Home_Page/datafile/histretSP.html) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High). [Federal Reserve History](https://www.federalreservehistory.org/-/media/Project/FedHistory/FedHistory/Documents/essaysPDFs/The-Great-Recession-_-Federal-Reserve-History.pdf) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High). Sterling’s multi-decade reserve loss was largely complete before the UK’s 1972–74 wipeout, which sources attribute to oil, inflation, property, and secondary banking. [Bank of England](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/quarterly-bulletin/1978/q2/the-secondary-banking-crisis-and-the-bank-of-englands-support-operations) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High). The same window in which the dollar’s reserve share fell from about 71 percent to about 58 percent saw large US equity gains. This suggests “de-dollarization equals US crash” has already failed as an out-of-sample prediction for a *gradual* reserve-share fade.

The fourth finding is that the street and the official sector do not forecast a BRICS-caused S&P crash. Goldman, BlackRock, Vanguard, the IMF, the Fed, the BIS, Eichengreen, and Prasad are dollar-bearish or “less exceptional” at most; they stay constructive or silent on a reserve-revolt crash. Krishnamurthy’s complete-loss model is +90 bp and about 8 percent real dollar depreciation, with the explicit line “Losing reserve currency status is not like a bank run.” [Stanford GSB](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/insights/dollars-dominance-eroding) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High). No Primary note publishes a percent S&P drawdown tied to a Treasury dump or a BRICS revolt.

The fifth finding is about what *can* crash US stocks, and how that will be mis-labeled. CAPE near 41 and a compressed ERP describe an expensive market. [Multpl](https://www.multpl.com/) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High). Fitch’s September 2026 35 percent figure is an *AI-sector* six-month stress test that “was not their base case,” not a BRICS path. [Business Insider / Fitch](https://www.businessinsider.com/ai-stock-crash-us-recession-global-stagnation-fitch-ratings-2026-9) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High). April 2025’s dollar-down-plus-stocks-down episode was a US tariff shock and recovered in weeks. [San Francisco Fed](https://www.frbsf.org/research-and-insights/publications/economic-letter/2025/10/market-reactions-to-tariff-announcements/) (accessed 2026-09-12, confidence: High). This suggests a 2026–2030 20 percent bear is plausible from valuation, AI-earnings, recession, tariffs, or a US institutional shock — and that a Kazan- or New Delhi-style communiqué in the same window will be used as the story even when the dollar’s sign says otherwise.

## Strategic Recommendations

1. **Do not sell the S&P on a BRICS or SCO headline.** The recorded BRICS equity book is under 1 percent of the US market, foreigners were net buyers through mid-2026, and no official house maps a summit communiqué onto a 20 percent index move. Evidence: [Treasury SB0482](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0482), [Treasury SB0606](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0606), [J.P. Morgan](https://www.jpmorgan.com/insights/global-research/currencies/de-dollarization).

2. **If you hedge “lost reserve status,” hedge duration and the dollar, not a crash put sized to 2008.** Krishnamurthy’s complete-loss counterfactual is about +90 bp and an 8 percent real dollar decline, not a 50 percent equity wipeout. A 20 percent S&P move from this cause needs a 100 bp *pure* discounting shock that observed official sales have not produced. Evidence: [Stanford GSB](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/insights/dollars-dominance-eroding), [Neely](https://files.stlouisfed.org/files/htdocs/wp/2017/2017-001.pdf), [Gormsen–Lazarus](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w34814/w34814.pdf).

3. **Watch US self-harm, not BRICS cohesion, as the crash channel that will be *named* de-dollarization.** CBO’s baseline is a grind, not a crash. The watch items that Eichengreen and Prasad name are a failed auction, a debt-ceiling interruption, or a completed Fed-independence break. India remains a documented veto on an anti-dollar union. Evidence: [CBO](https://www.cbo.gov/publication/62105), [Prasad, Foreign Affairs](https://www.foreignaffairs.com/united-states/top-dollar-currency-prasad), [Reuters / Jaishankar](https://www.reuters.com/world/india/brics-have-no-interest-weakening-usd-indian-foreign-minister-says-2024-12-07/).

4. **Treat a 20 percent bear and a 30 percent crash as different objects.** A 20 percent closing decline is S&P DJI’s bear rule and has happened without reserve-status loss (2022). A 30 percent or 1987/2008 event in this sample required a financial-system crash or a large earnings recession, and in 2008 the dollar *rose*. Do not use “崩” for both. Evidence: [S&P DJI / Silverblatt](https://www.cnbc.com/2022/06/13/sp-500-is-in-official-bear-market-according-to-sp-dow-jones-indices.html), [McCauley–McGuire, BIS](https://www.bis.org/publications/dollar-appreciation-2008-safe-haven-carry-trades-dollar-shortage-and-overhedging_1.pdf).

5. **Use the falsifiable markers before updating.** A ≥20 percent claim from *this cause* needs a Fed/BIS/IMF-attributed 100-plus bp official-sale discounting shock and/or ~$2 trillion of identified net foreign equity *transactions*. Until those prints appear, keep the base case. This recommendation is synthesis; the markers are constructed from the elasticities cited above.

- **Low-confidence claims:** Linear application of Beltran’s 1994–2010 *dollar* coefficient to a $3.5 trillion 2026 dump; the 21 percent official-equity Gabaix–Koijen thought experiment; Goldman’s 66 percent ADR-delisting barometer as recapped by Benzinga.
- **Unresolved conflicts:** Official-flow yield elasticities disagree by an order of magnitude (Neely 19–32 bp for $3 trillion versus JPM >33 bp per $300 billion versus Beltran 40–60 bp per $100 billion monthly). ACM versus CR term premia (0.68 vs 1.33 percent). April 2025 (dollar, bonds, and equities down together; BIS: hedging / SF Fed: tariffs) versus 2008/2022 (dollar up, equities down). Krishnamurthy wealth-loss recaps ($18T vs $29T).
- **Domain risks to monitor:** a new April-2025-style “Sell America” regime in which the dollar no longer hedges US risk; a completed Fed-independence break; a Gulf SWF rotation that is larger than the $257 billion identifiable Treasury book; an Indian flip; a surprise RMB convertibility opening; an AI-earnings bust that coincides with a BRICS communiqué.

## Critique pass

Three ways this synthesis could be wrong. First, April 2025 may be a *regime break*, not a tariff one-off. If the dollar has lost its intra-crisis hedge, a future official sale that would once have been absorbed by flight-to-quality could print the triple decline again and travel farther. BIS and BlackRock both refuse that reading so far; the year then closed +16–18 percent. The refusal could be late. Second, Cayman and London custody may hide a BRICS beneficial book several times the $429 billion residence print. No source found a number. If the hidden book is Western private capital, it is not a BRICS policy tool; if it is official, the equity channel is understated. Third, Western official and bulge-bracket sources have an incentive to say the dollar is fine. The counterweight used here is the same sources’ *own* elasticities and the 2015–16 / 2020 / April 2025 event studies, plus the failed 2010–2015 crash-prophet record — not their adjectives. Alternative explanation that still fits the prints: the US market is expensive, so *any* shock can produce a 20 percent bear, and BRICS is the narrative that will be attached after the fact. That is consistent with the fifth finding and does not rescue the causal claim.
