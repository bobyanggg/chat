# Company-Specific and Sector News — US Tech, ~20 July–20 August 2026

> **Assumptions:** Axis is idiosyncratic news versus macro duration. Research date 2026-08-20. Figures are as reported in cited sources; private-company OpenAI/Anthropic numbers are press-cited, not filings.

## Company-Specific and Sector News

The late-July through 20 August 2026 window was not a uniform Mag7 earnings wipeout. It was a split print: several megacaps and chip names beat, a smaller set missed earnings or guided light, and the 18–19 August tape then sold the most crowded AI-hardware names even where the last company print had been strong. That pattern is the main fact that distinguishes this axis from a simple “everyone missed” story.

### Mag7 and AI-infrastructure earnings, late July–early August

Alphabet reported on 22 July. The company’s own release showed revenue of $119.8 billion, up 24% year over year, Google Cloud revenue of $24.8 billion, up 82%, operating income of $40.8 billion, and diluted EPS of $9.11, which included a net other-income gain of about $98–99 billion, primarily unrealized gains on equity securities. [Alphabet Q2 2026 earnings release](https://s206.q4cdn.com/479360582/files/doc_financials/2026/q2/2026q2-alphabet-earnings-release.pdf) (accessed 2026-08-20, **Primary**, confidence: High). CNBC and Investopedia both recorded a revenue-and-EPS beat versus Visible Alpha/Street estimates, with Cloud the driver, and both recorded the stock falling in extended or next-day trading because management lifted 2026 capital-expenditure guidance to $195–205 billion from $180–190 billion and because free cash flow turned negative. [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/22/google-earnings-q2-goog-live-updates.html) (accessed 2026-08-20, **Established**, confidence: High); [Investopedia](https://www.investopedia.com/big-tech-earnings-live-q2-2026-googl-tsla-ibm-txn-now-12024335) (accessed 2026-08-20, **Established**, confidence: High). Quartz put Q2 capex at $44.9 billion and stated Alphabet raised $49.6 billion of equity in June plus $20.3 billion of senior unsecured notes in the quarter, matching the company’s own equity-and-notes disclosure. [Quartz](https://qz.com/alphabet-google-second-quarter-earnings-revenue-cloud-072226) (accessed 2026-08-20, **Established**, confidence: High); [Alphabet Q2 2026 earnings release](https://s206.q4cdn.com/479360582/files/doc_financials/2026/q2/2026q2-alphabet-earnings-release.pdf) (accessed 2026-08-20, **Primary**). Invezz and The Next Web both described the post-print decline as a capex/FCF reaction rather than a revenue miss, with Invezz citing a more-than-3.5% premarket drop and The Next Web citing about 5% after hours. [Invezz](https://invezz.com/news/2026/07/23/alphabet-stock-drops-as-higher-capex-negative-fcf-overshadow-google-c-andloud-surge/) (accessed 2026-08-20, **Established**, confidence: Medium); [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/alphabet-q2-2026-capex-205bn-cloud-82) (accessed 2026-08-20, **Established**, confidence: Medium). Barchart later wrote that Alphabet “missed on the bottom line.” That claim conflicts with the official EPS figure and with CNBC/Investopedia’s beat language; the $9.11 print is not a comparable operating EPS number because of the equity-securities gain. [Barchart](https://www.barchart.com/story/news/3610128/heres-what-magnificent-7-earnings-tell-us-about-the-ai-trade-in-2026) (accessed 2026-08-20, **Established**, confidence: Low on the miss claim).

Tesla reported the same day. CNBC recorded adjusted EPS of $0.33 versus $0.51 expected, revenue of $28.24 billion versus $25.71 billion expected, operating margin of 1.4% versus 4.1% a year earlier, and free cash flow of negative $1.1 billion. [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/22/tesla-tsla-q2-2026-earnings-report.html) (accessed 2026-08-20, **Established**, confidence: High). Investopedia similarly recorded an EPS miss (33 cents versus 55 cents Visible Alpha) with a revenue beat and after-hours selling. [Investopedia](https://www.investopedia.com/big-tech-earnings-live-q2-2026-googl-tsla-ibm-txn-now-12024335) (accessed 2026-08-20, **Established**). That is a genuine fundamental miss on profitability and cash conversion, not a multiple-only event, though Tesla is only loosely an AI-infrastructure name.

Meta reported on 29 July. The company’s release showed revenue of $60.80 billion, up 28%, diluted EPS of $6.18, operating income down 8% to $18.78 billion, capex of $31.08 billion, and free cash flow of $784 million. Q3 revenue was guided to $61–64 billion. Full-year 2026 capex, including principal payments on finance leases, was narrowed to $130–145 billion from $125–145 billion, which raised the floor rather than cutting the ceiling. [Meta IR](https://investor.atmeta.com/investor-news/press-release-details/2026/Meta-Reports-Second-Quarter-2026-Results/default.aspx) (accessed 2026-08-20, **Primary**, confidence: High). CNBC compared those figures with LSEG: EPS $6.18 versus $7.22 expected (a miss), revenue $60.80 billion versus $60.17 billion (a beat), Q3 midpoint $62.5 billion versus LSEG expected guidance of $63.15 billion (light), and FCF down from $8.55 billion a year earlier. [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/29/meta-q2-earnings-report-2026.html) (accessed 2026-08-20, **Established**, confidence: High). Barchart later said Meta had fallen about 32% from its 52-week high and tied the decline to the light Q3 guide amid heavy AI spend. [Barchart](https://www.barchart.com/story/news/3952857/meta-stock-sinks-32-but-analysts-see-a-38-rebound) (accessed 2026-08-20, **Established**, confidence: Medium). Meta is the cleanest Mag7 case of a true earnings-and-guidance disappointment inside the window.

Microsoft reported fiscal Q4 on 29 July and was received as a winner. CNBC recorded a top-and-bottom-line beat, Azure growth of 43%, Azure fiscal-2026 revenue above $100 billion, quarterly capex and finance leases of $41 billion, up 69%, and a next-day surge that other outlets put near 15% and as Microsoft’s best day since 2008. CFO Amy Hood said calendar-2026 investment plans were unchanged; extending data-center and office useful lives from 15 to 25 years would shift more future leases from finance to operating classification and therefore print calendar-2026 capex at about $175 billion rather than the prior ~$190 billion presentation. Hood also said Microsoft should remain free-cash-flow positive in fiscal 2027. [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/29/microsoft-msft-q4-earnings-report-2026.html) (accessed 2026-08-20, **Established**, confidence: High); [Microsoft FY26 Q4 earnings call remarks](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/investor/events/fy-2026/earnings-fy-2026-q4) (accessed 2026-08-20, **Primary**, confidence: High); [Barchart](https://www.barchart.com/story/news/3610128/heres-what-magnificent-7-earnings-tell-us-about-the-ai-trade-in-2026) (accessed 2026-08-20, **Established**, confidence: Medium on the +15% day). This was not a capex cut. It was an accounting reclassification that some headlines could misread as a pullback.

Amazon reported on 30 July and was also received as a winner. CNBC recorded adjusted EPS of $1.97 versus $1.82 LSEG, revenue of $200.61 billion versus $196.47 billion, AWS of $42.2 billion versus $40.54 billion StreetAccount (37% growth versus 31% expected), quarterly capex of $54.2 billion, trailing-twelve-month free cash flow of negative $7.6 billion, and a more-than-10% after-hours rally. CEO Andy Jassy raised 2026 cash capex to about $220 billion from $200 billion, citing higher memory costs, and said even that amount would not meet 2026 or 2027 demand. AWS backlog was $496 billion. Amazon’s current-quarter revenue guide of $197–202 billion was below LSEG’s $204.1 billion, which Amazon attributed to Prime Day moving into Q2. [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/30/amazon-amzn-q2-earnings-report-2026.html) (accessed 2026-08-20, **Established**, confidence: High). Morningstar commentary, as carried by TradingView/Stocktwits, said Jassy “made a strong case for AI data center ROI” and that Amazon’s results helped Alphabet recover its post-earnings decline. [TradingView / Stocktwits](https://www.tradingview.com/news/stocktwits:02530426b094b:0-amazon-and-microsoft-ace-wall-street-s-ai-test-analyst-warns-mag-seven-now-faces-a-higher-burden-of-proof/) (accessed 2026-08-20, **Established**, confidence: Medium). Amazon therefore combined a genuine FCF deterioration and a raised capex bill with a market-positive ROI narrative.

Apple reported fiscal Q3 on 30 July. The company posted revenue of $109.4 billion, up 16%, and diluted EPS of $2.02. [Apple Newsroom](https://www.apple.com/newsroom/2026/07/apple-reports-third-quarter-results/) (accessed 2026-08-20, **Primary**, confidence: High). CNBC said September-quarter revenue growth was guided to 9–11% versus LSEG expected growth of about 12%, with Tim Cook calling memory a “hundred-year flood” and warning that supply constraints on advanced SoC nodes and DRAM would bite harder sequentially. [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/30/apple-earnings-live-updates.html) (accessed 2026-08-20, **Established**, confidence: High). MacRumors and Computerworld independently quoted the same constraint language. [MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2026/07/30/apple-iphone-mac-ipad-supply-constraints/) (accessed 2026-08-20, **Established**, confidence: High); [Computerworld](https://www.computerworld.com/article/4203974/apples-tim-cook-era-ends-with-a-record-109b-quarter.html) (accessed 2026-08-20, **Established**, confidence: Medium). Apple’s quarter was not an EPS miss; the disappointment was forward supply and memory-cost guidance. That is idiosyncratic, and it is also a positive demand signal for memory producers, which matters when those same memory stocks later sold off.

Nvidia had not yet reported inside this window. 24/7 Wall St. said Nvidia would report on 26 August. [24/7 Wall St.](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/08/19/intel-and-amd-fall-4-nvidia-unchanged-as-chip-selloff-defies-bond-yield-relief/) (accessed 2026-08-20, **Established**, confidence: Medium). There is therefore no Nvidia earnings miss to assign to 18–19 August.

### OpenAI Q2 losses and the 19 August Broadcom/AMD claim

Multiple Established outlets, all citing the Wall Street Journal and people familiar with investor materials, reported that OpenAI told investors Q2 revenue rose 18% sequentially to $6.7 billion from $5.7 billion, while the operating loss including stock-based compensation widened to $12.3 billion from $9.3 billion. Anthropic was reported to have more than doubled revenue to $11.6 billion and to have posted a small adjusted operating profit, overtaking OpenAI on quarterly sales for the first time. [Yahoo Finance summarizing WSJ](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/openai-q2-growth-trails-anthropic-102250259.html) (accessed 2026-08-20, **Established**, confidence: High on the figures as reported); [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/deeptech/openai-trails-anthropic-in-q2-revenue-as-growth-slows-and-losses-deepen) (accessed 2026-08-20, **Established**); [CoinDesk](https://www.coindesk.com/markets/2026/08/19/openai-trails-anthropic-as-losses-deepen-and-altman-pauses-frontier-ai-training) (accessed 2026-08-20, **Established**). CoinDesk dated the WSJ report to Tuesday 18 August. These are reported private-company figures, not filings.

The causal claim that those OpenAI numbers were “the” 19 August Broadcom/AMD drag does not hold up against the better Broadcom coverage. CNBC, SiliconANGLE, and BNN Bloomberg all attributed Broadcom’s about-5% decline on Wednesday 19 August to Marvell’s Google custom-silicon/TPU-attach filing, not to OpenAI. [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/19/marvell-google-ai-chips.html) (accessed 2026-08-20, **Established**, confidence: High); [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2026/08/19/marvell-shares-jump-9-8-on-google-chip-design-deal/) (accessed 2026-08-20, **Established**); [BNN Bloomberg](https://www.bnnbloomberg.ca/business/technology/2026/08/19/marvell-gives-google-option-to-buy-us122-billion-stake-in-custom-chip-deal/) (accessed 2026-08-20, **Established**). AMD’s 19 August decline of about 3.7–4% was described by 24/7 Wall St. as parallel profit-taking in elevated-YTD chip names with no single-name event, by Invezz as high-multiple selectivity plus a recent $4.75 billion AMD bond deal, and by TS2 as a valuation collision (trailing P/E near 120x). [24/7 Wall St.](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/08/19/intel-and-amd-fall-4-nvidia-unchanged-as-chip-selloff-defies-bond-yield-relief/) (accessed 2026-08-20, **Established**, confidence: Medium); [Invezz](https://invezz.com/news/2026/08/19/why-are-intel-and-amd-stocks-falling-on-wednesday/) (accessed 2026-08-20, **Established**); [TS2.tech](https://ts2.tech/en/amd-stock-falls-3-7-as-4x-ai-efficiency-estimate-meets-a-120x-multiple/) (accessed 2026-08-20, **Low**). Odaily, a Low-tier crypto outlet, did tie an overnight AI-hardware washout — including SanDisk, SK Hynix, Micron, Coherent, and CoreWeave — to the OpenAI Q2 print plus Treasury yields, but it did not isolate Broadcom and AMD as the 19 August expression. [Odaily](https://www.odaily.news/en/post/5212574) (accessed 2026-08-20, **Low**, confidence: Low). Yahoo’s WSJ write-up named Nvidia and Oracle, not Broadcom and AMD, as the suppliers most exposed to OpenAI’s compute commitments. [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/technology/ai/articles/openai-q2-growth-trails-anthropic-102250259.html) (accessed 2026-08-20, **Established**). Synthesis: the OpenAI loss-widening print is corroborated as 18 August news and is a plausible contributor to 18 August AI-hardware selling. It is not corroborated as the specific Broadcom/AMD 19 August catalyst.

### Memory and HDD on 18–19 August: why they led

On 18 August, 24/7 Wall St. published two same-day accounts of the same tape. One said Micron was down about 5%, SanDisk about 6%, Western Digital about 7%, and Seagate about 6%, with year-to-date gains of 255%, 653%, 211%, and 262% respectively, and argued there was “no news about memory pricing, supply, demand, or customer orders” — a valuation reset from higher Treasury yields. [24/7 Wall St., rates piece](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/08/18/micron-technology-falls-5-sandisk-sinks-6-western-digital-drops-7-as-higher-rates-test-the-memory-boom/) (accessed 2026-08-20, **Established**, confidence: Medium). The other, timestamped 12:50 p.m. ET, put SanDisk down 9.1%, Micron 7.3%, and Western Digital 5.3% and named as trigger a Wall Street Journal footnotes analysis that nine large tech companies held about $3 trillion of off-balance-sheet commitments, mostly AI-related, versus about $600 billion of trailing capex. It added that Anthropic and OpenAI revenue figures circulating over the weekend had also come in below some private whispers, and it repeated that “there is no memory-specific bad news today” and that Micron’s last guide remained $50 billion plus or minus $1 billion at about 86% gross margin. It also recorded that SanDisk had entered the session up 44% on the week. [24/7 Wall St., WSJ piece](https://247wallst.com/investing/2026/08/18/wsj-report-sends-memory-stocks-down-sandisk-down-9-micron-down-7-western-digital-down-5/) (accessed 2026-08-20, **Established**, confidence: Medium). Yahoo Finance carried the same WSJ-trigger version. [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/wsj-report-sends-memory-stocks-165044072.html) (accessed 2026-08-20, **Established**).

The WSJ analysis itself is dated 16 August 2026, 9:00 p.m. ET, under the headline that Big Tech’s AI spending is $3 trillion higher than it seems, covering unstarted data-center leases and purchase commitments. [WSJ](https://www.wsj.com/tech/ai/why-big-techs-ai-spending-is-3-trillion-higher-than-it-seems-e1067bb2) (accessed 2026-08-20, **Established**; full text paywalled, confidence: High on existence and headline figures via concordant secondaries). FlowingData and TipRanks both reproduced the $3 trillion versus $600 billion capex comparison and the authors Peter Rudegeair and Peter Santilli. TipRanks added that Alphabet’s purchase commitments reached $811 billion as of 30 June, up from $332 billion three months earlier. [FlowingData](https://flowingdata.com/2026/08/19/trillions-of-dollars-in-ai-spending-not-in-the-balance-sheet-yet/) (accessed 2026-08-20, **Established**, confidence: High); [TipRanks](https://www.tipranks.com/news/big-tech-has-3-trillion-in-ai-commitments-hidden-off-the-balance-sheet) (accessed 2026-08-20, **Established**, confidence: Medium).

Taken together, the 18 August memory/HDD leadership is best described as multiple compression and profit-taking in the most extended AI-hardware cohort after a WSJ obligation story and a private-lab revenue story, not as a new miss on memory pricing or unit demand. Company fundamentals from the last cycle were still strong. The earlier 28 July / 5 August storage “beat-and-drop” showed that this group was already being held to a higher bar. Apple’s memory-cost comments two weeks earlier pointed the other way on physical demand.

### Marvell and Google TPU, 19 August, as a counterexample

Marvell’s 8-K states that on 29 July 2026 it entered a commercial agreement with Google to develop custom products attaching to the TPU ecosystem (AI inference accelerators, storage and network interface controllers, memory interface controllers, and near-memory compute) and that on 18 August 2026 it issued Google a warrant for up to 58,970,907 shares at $206.58, vesting partly on time and partly at $500 million of custom-product revenue per tranche through fiscal 2033. ### Distinguishing fundamental disappointment from multiple compression

Fundamental disappointment inside the window is concentrated in Meta (EPS miss, light Q3 revenue guide, FCF collapse), Tesla (EPS miss, negative FCF), Apple (in-quarter beat, light September guide on memory and SoC constraints), Alphabet’s cash-flow and funding mix despite an operating beat, and Amazon’s negative TTM FCF even as AWS and the stock responded well. AMD’s print was a consensus beat with a whisper miss. Microsoft was a fundamental beat. Nvidia and Broadcom did not report. Micron’s last guide, from June, remained a blowout. Sandisk, Western Digital, and Seagate printed strong results in late July and early August and still saw post-print selling, which is already multiple-bar behavior.

The 18–19 August leadership of SanDisk, Micron, Western Digital, and Seagate is not explained by a new earnings miss. Same-day Established coverage said there was no memory-specific bad news and pointed to year-to-date and week-to-date extension, Treasury yields, the WSJ $3 trillion commitment story, and private-lab revenue coming in below whispers. OpenAI’s widening losses are real as reported, but they are a sector-narrative shock to AI-hardware multiples, not a change in DRAM contract pricing. Marvell’s 19 August gain shows that idiosyncratic positive news could still reprice a name higher on the same day Broadcom and AMD fell. The residual after those company facts is multiple compression in the most crowded, highest-YTD AI-hardware cohort, overlapping with duration but not reducible to a fresh fundamental miss in memory.
